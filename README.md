# app

The Forest app is your control panel for Forest: where you create your profiles, get verified, post, keep up with your feedback and messages, and connect your AI. Your profiles and everything they post live on hosts, open for anyone to read; the app reads them and writes for you. Your device keeps addresses and settings, never your seed, its 24 words, a key, a stamp or a message: the full list is under Behind the scenes.

Your seed is what all your profiles come from. It's kept by your passkey, in the password manager you choose. To act, you unlock: the app asks your passkey for the seed, and you allow it with your device's unlock (face, fingerprint or PIN). A button that will ask shows the unlock icon, a fingerprint, before its word, but on Start and its sheets, where every button asks, and on Get verified's cards and a list of profiles to tick, where the card or the row is the choice itself.

The app is open source, so anyone can run one. The Forest Foundation runs this one, at https://app.forest.foundation. It's a web app for now; phone apps may come later, from the same code.

## How it works

Three places: **Home**, **Notifications** and **You**. On a phone they are three tabs at the bottom; on a laptop, a sidebar, with Messages and your profiles too: Profiles, with a small mint New at its right for a new one, then every profile, in Home's order, the sidebar scrolling on its own when they run long. On a laptop every screen is one column, centred, but Home's cards, which fill the width, and Messages, in two panes. A small choice or edit (Appearance, a passkey, naming someone, a role, what the checks keep, the pickers) opens as a sheet from the bottom on a phone, and on a laptop as a dialog in the middle, over the dimmed page, which Escape or a click outside closes; steps and lists are screens of their own.

1. **Start.** One button, Start. On a device that knows your passkey, Start asks for it and takes you in: to Home, or to where setup was left. Once a device has used a passkey, Start asks for that one alone. On a new device, Start asks First time here? Yes, I'm new makes your passkey (your password manager keeps it, and with it your seed), then Get verified. I use Forest on another device asks for one you have: one synced here, or one on another device, such as your phone, through the QR code your browser shows. Every passkey you've added opens the same profiles. Let in by a passkey on another device, the app offers once to save one on this device too, and from then on this device asks that one. When no passkey answers, No passkey found offers Try again, or Type my 24 words: type the 24 words You shows, in order, and the app makes a new passkey that opens your seed from then on, as one added in You does, and you're in. Continue waits for all 24; words that can't be used say why in one line before any passkey is made; words that open a different Forest from the one this device lists are refused, and Forget this device comes first, so one device never mixes two seeds.

2. **Get verified.** The first time, a verifier checks that you're a real person it hasn't seen before, and signs a stamp to your seed, so people know you're real. A page you come back to, with two cards, Selfie and ID, each one tap while it's to do, with its price and what it gives:
   - **Selfie, Free.** A quick selfie, opened at once, with free credits for posts and messages.
   - **ID, $5 one time.** Your ID and a selfie: ten times the free credits, stronger trust (indexes can count it as stronger), and you're only charged if it passes; it helps fund the foundation. The tap goes straight to Stripe, after one question: whether the verifier signed you an ID stamp already (one a person, ever); if so, the app keeps it and you pay nothing. Else you pay the verifier directly, through Stripe, and Stripe's page goes straight on to the ID check, with nothing to tap between; the app never sees your card. Back from Stripe without paying, the card is as it was.

   While the ID can be tapped, What the checks keep, a quiet link under the cards, opens the verifier's words for its checks, word for word as it states them, so they're one tap away before anyone pays.

   Both run with Didit, the identity-check company our verifier uses, in the same tab as the app, and Didit sends you back to the app when you're done. The page that went to Didit held your seed; the one you come back to doesn't, so it asks your passkey once more, by itself where the browser lets it, else with Continue; then it waits for the stamp and brings you back to Get verified, that card Verified, on a mint chip, no longer a tap. From Start, Continue then goes on to your first profile (at the foot on a phone, under the cards on a laptop), and back is gone; from You or Home, Get verified has Back and no Continue. With the stamp comes a gift of credits, which pay the host for what you write; the cards name credits only where the verifier gives them. Both go into your Profiles folder, a private folder only your seed opens, so any device you start on finds them.

   When something goes wrong, one warning at the top says so, and a card that can't help you any more is greyed, its name alone:
   - a selfie that doesn't pass: "That selfie didn't pass. Try again, or use your ID.";
   - a face Didit has seen verified before, most likely yours on a new passkey: "A similar face is already verified. Verify with your ID instead.", the selfie greyed, since it would match again;
   - an ID that already verified another Forest: "This ID is already verified with another passkey. Nothing was charged.", the ID greyed;
   - an ID check that doesn't pass, with tries left: "That ID check didn't pass. Try again.";
   - a paid one that ends with no stamp, its three tries used or nothing held on your card any more: "Your tries are used up. Nothing was charged. Pay again to continue."

   The next tap opens a new check. After a face seen before or an ID already used, from Start, I already use Forest, under the cards, is for someone who made a passkey here by mistake: it asks for the passkey they use, adds this one to it, so it opens their profiles from then on, and they're in. From You or Home it never shows: there you're inside a working Forest, and moving to another is Forget this device and Start with its passkey, a step taken on purpose. Going back loses nothing: pick the same check again and it goes on where it was; switch, and a payment you made waits for the ID check. Close the page midway, and Start picks up where you were. You can add the other check later, in You.

   Then your first profile: every market as a tile, live and beta alike, two to a row (on a laptop, four): its icon at the top left, Beta in small quiet text at the top right of a beta one, its name at the bottom, its first word small over the rest where another market in the directory starts with it too (Stays over Work); the search finds the whole name. Tap one: its sheet, the first sentence of the market's own description under its title, then Create beside your role (Tutor, Student), and the app makes it, or Later. A beta market's sheet has a Beta chip in its title, and says once: "Beta: it works, at your own risk, and may change." Then Connect your AI (8), or Skip.

3. **Home.** Your profiles. On a laptop, beside the title, how you're verified, once, since every profile of yours carries the same checks: a badge for each, the same forest square, a face being scanned for the selfie check, an ID card for the ID check, each saying so when tapped; a check you haven't done shows as its badge's outline, dashed and muted, and a tap opens Get verified. On a phone they live in You and on each profile's page. Then one card per profile, with its avatar (its market's icon on its market's color, solid when it offers, light when it requests), how many things are new on it (messages, reviews, vouches and reports) in a small green count on the avatar's corner, its market in bold and its role under it, and at its right its rating over how many reviews it has, or No reviews; every card one height, rated or not. New profile is always in reach while a market's role has no profile of yours: on a phone, a round forest + right after the title; on a laptop, New profile at the top right of Home. Profiles with something new come first (a message, a review, a vouch or a report, or a listing to update), then the ones you used most recently on this device (opened, posted from, written from). On a laptop, the cards fill the width, as many columns of about 300px as fit, each row one height, a name too long ending short. A profile's own page keeps its badges in its header, and other people's profiles show theirs everywhere. At the top, Search Forest opens the index. To make a new profile, tap New profile, pick a market, then Create beside a role, and unlock: the app makes it, proving it comes from a verified seed without saying which; a role you have shows a check, and opens it. While it's made, while a check is waited for, and while your messages are read, the loader shows once the wait passes half a second, alone in the middle; nothing else waits behind it. Share hands out a profile's page on the index, for anyone who wants to message or review it. Profiles made on another device show here after one unlock. A profile whose market is gone, one the directory no longer lists (a test profile from the old markets), is forgotten by the device and shown nowhere; nothing is deleted at the host.

4. **Listings.** Post an offer or a request from a profile, as its role posts. The form is the market's own file, read as it is: a question that depends on earlier answers shows once they hold, and goes when a changed answer means it no longer applies, so goods asks what is sold, and for clothes who wears them and their sizes, and for a child's, their age. A listing can have photos, a price (and what it's per, when the market says, such as per hour), numbers, the market's choices, several where it allows, a long list from a sheet with a search, a place and hours, each on a screen of its own. A number shows its unit after it, one or many as the number says (1 night, 3 nights; 4 people), in the form and wherever the listing shows it. A long list shows each value by its name, never a code (Spanish, never es), and its search finds them by name. In a beta market, the form says once, at its top, "Beta: it works, at your own risk, and may change." A choice that takes one value looks like one: side by side, or chips where picking one unpicks the other, or on its sheet rows with a radio mark, a tap picking it and closing the sheet; one that takes several shows a tick on each picked, and its sheet tick boxes and Done. The market's file says which, for every market alike; a listing can carry a contact. A place is set on a map, on a screen of its own: an area or an exact spot. An area is a circle you tap or drag into place and size, from the market's own size up to 50 km. Its centre is moved a few hundred metres from where you put it, once, and kept on the device, and an area put within 800 metres of a centre already kept takes that one, so your listings never average out to your home; the spot you put it on stays inside the circle, which glides to where it will be posted. An exact spot is a pin you drop, posted exactly there, for anyone to see. A field that holds several places, such as where a guest would stay, takes several: each its own row, set on its own screen, with a remove button, and Add another place under them; the listing shows them all. Your location only centres the map: the app reads it for a new place where your browser already allows it, or when you tap the map's own button. A contact (a phone, a handle, an email) is public, and the same contact on two listings links them. You can edit or delete a listing. When the market changes and a listing no longer fits it, its card says it needs an update, and so does Notifications, and it opens with what still fits filled in.

5. **Notifications.** Every review, vouch and report about any of your profiles, under Today and Earlier, newest first, in full, each with who wrote it, their avatar and your name for them or their key, with nothing unlocked; the new ones are marked until you've seen them; a tap on who wrote it opens their profile (7), and anywhere else that profile's Reviews. And a notice for each of your own listings and reviews a market outgrew (it needs an update: the market changed), which opens it with what still fits filled in; posted, the notice goes. Its tab counts both, and new messages too; at its top, Messages, with how many are new. Each profile's page has a header of four parts: its market, small, in its color; its role, big; its badges right after the role; and the rating an index gives it (ours here) with how many reviews, or with no score yet what came (1 vouch), on a pill that opens the reviews it received: the score large, how many, which index rated it, and what that index counted about it, newest first, each with who wrote it, by your name for them or their key, and opening in full, with From, a row with who wrote it that opens their profile, then Message, Review back and Report; "No reviews yet" when nothing came. Then two tabs: its offers or requests, each a card with its price, and Reviews: the reviews, vouches and reports the profile wrote, under one quiet line, each with who it's about, by name or key; tap one to edit or delete it, or who it's about to open their profile. You can start a review from a profile's Reviews tab, a thread, someone's profile, or a review you received; from a profile, it first asks who: someone the profile has a conversation with, or their link or address. Then one of three: a score from 1 to 10, in tenths, with the market's own questions; a vouch, that they're real and honest, without a score; or a report, that they're fake or a scam. None names which of their listings it's about. Each is about someone on the other side of the same market (in a market with one role, like language exchange, the same role), never one of your own profiles, and the app checks that before you unlock, in one line. Written again about the same person, a review, vouch or report goes in place of the one before: an index counts one of each.

6. **Messages.** Messages, from the round button at the top of Notifications (on a laptop, the sidebar), hold every profile's conversations, newest first, with a chip for each of your profiles, a dot in its market's color and its market and role (Goods seller), to show its own; the same button at a profile's top opens them on its chip. They show how many are new with nothing unlocked, and on which of your profiles: who wrote one shows only once it's opened. Opening them takes one unlock, unless your seed is held, and then they open by themselves as they come. Each conversation is one row: their avatar, your name for them or their key, when, and the last message, bold with a dot while it's new; who they are is on their profile, a tap away. On a phone the tabs stay under Messages, Notifications picked; a conversation has the screen to itself. Each thread shows who it's with (your name for them or their key, how they're verified, their market and role; a tap opens their profile) and to which of your profiles; reply there, or review them. On a laptop, Messages is two panes: the conversations on the left, with their chips, and the one open on the right; a phone shows one at a time. While they open, the loader sits alone in the middle of the screen, across both panes on a laptop. A new message, from the pencil at the top, goes to an address or a profile's page on the index, one of your own profiles too, from your profile on the other side of their market (in a market with one role, like language exchange, the same role), or a new one when you have none. A message is locked so only they can read it, and paid in tiny amounts from your profile's credits. Every message a profile sends, from the app or your AI, keeps a locked copy in its folder, so on any device the app shows the whole conversation, and your AI everything since you connected it.

7. **Other people.** Tap another profile wherever it shows (a thread's header, a review's From, who a review is about, a notification) and its page opens, marked Not yours: its header as your own profiles have it, then who they are to you, your name for them or else their short key, on a chip with a pencil; three numbers, their rating and how many reviews, how many listings they have (2 requests), how many reviews they've written; Message and Review; the reviews they received; and See everything on the index, their page there, in a new tab. It's read from the host and the index as you open it, and kept nowhere. A profile shows as its short key until you name it: the chip opens a sheet with their full key, which you can copy, and a Name field; Save, and that name shows in place of the key wherever they appear, for you alone (Only you see it), never beside it; Show the key instead takes it back. Saving asks for an unlock unless your seed is held; the names live in your Profiles folder, so every device you unlock on gets them.

8. **Your AI.** To connect it, add a connector in your AI app with the address https://mcp.forest.foundation (Connect your AI, right after your first profile and in You's Your AI, shows how: copy the address, add it in Claude or ChatGPT as a connector, sign in when it asks), and if it asks how to sign in, choose to sign in now: Claude offers No sign-in first, and with it Claude can read but never act. The first time your AI acts, it sends you to the app's connect page, which shows the AI app's name and, under it, the site it sends you back to: that site is what says which app it really is. Tick the profiles it may act for, unlock once, and you're back in your chat. If this browser isn't in yet, Start comes first, and, if you're new, getting verified and your first profile; then the same page. Each profile ticked hands it an access key that can read, post, read the inbox and message; the AI never gets your seed, a main key or your addresses, and the app never shows the key or keeps it. Your AI app's own settings choose which of its tools it uses.

9. **You.** One column, on a phone and a laptop alike. At the top, how you're verified: Selfie and ID, each its badge and Verified, or for one you haven't done its badge's outline, dashed, what it gives (the ID: stronger trust, and ten times the free credits) and Add, either opening Get verified. Then a row for each of these, opening its own screen:
   - **Passkeys:** each passkey a row, by the name you gave it on this device, else where it lives, read from the id it sent as it was made (Apple, Google, 1Password, Microsoft, Samsung), else Passkey, with Apple's or Google's logo, else a key; two of one name are told apart by a number (Apple 2); your first, the one your seed comes from, marked Main. Tap one for its name, the Name field and Save, and Remove at the left, in red: any but Main, which the app never removes. Add one, with one unlock, in another password manager, on a second phone or on a security key (the passkeys this device knows are excluded, so your password manager doesn't keep a second one). Under If you lose every passkey, Your 24 words: a sheet warns that anyone who sees them can act as you, then Show words asks your passkey, even when the seed is held, and shows your seed as 24 words, with one line: never type them into a site or app you don't trust. Nothing stores them; they show only while that screen is open.
   - **Your AI:** your AI apps, each with how many profiles it acts for and since when; each opens to change which profiles it acts for, keeping at least one, or Disconnect it: it stops at once, and what it wrote stays. Connect another. One you connected from another browser shows here after your next unlock, found by itself.
   - **Appearance:** Auto (your device's), Light or Dark, on a sheet.
   - **About Forest:** Privacy, Terms and the source code, each in a new tab, and the version.

   At the foot, Forget this device (your passkey stays in your manager).

## Behind the scenes

- **Your seed** is your first passkey's PRF secret: a value the passkey gives only after you unlock, the same 32 bytes on every device it syncs to. After an unlock, the app holds it in the page's memory 5 minutes since the last action, or until the page closes, or until Forget. What the page does by itself, opening messages again as new ones come, uses the seed held but doesn't count as an action. A page only hidden (another window over it, another tab, the phone locked) keeps it, and an action already running finishes first. It is never stored on a device, nor anything secret made from it. Each passkey you add keeps a copy on the host, locked so only that passkey opens it.
- **Your 24 words** are your seed itself, as the standard spells it (`exportWords`): BIP39's words for its 32 bytes, without BIP39's passphrase step. Passkeys shows them after a fresh unlock, even when the seed is held, and only while their screen is open; they live in the page's memory while it is, never its history, the seed they came from is dropped at once, and a reload there opens Passkeys. Type my 24 words, under Start's No passkey found, reads them back (`importWords`) and checks, before any passkey is made: all 24, each in its place; verified, a stamp in their Profiles folder, whose wallet pays for the new passkey's folder; and the Forest this device lists, if it lists one. Words that spell an added passkey's own seed are followed one step, to the seed it opens. Then the app makes a passkey and adds it as below, so from then on it opens your seed.
- **Names you give others** live in one locked entry of your Profiles folder, the app's own, at `names`: `{ "names": [{ "address": <their profile's address>, "name": <your name for it> }] }`, locked to the folder's own key alone, so only your seed opens it and any device you unlock on gets them. Saving reads the entry as the host holds it, changes one name and writes it again, paid from your wallet; taking back the last deletes it. The standard leaves paths like it to the app that writes them, and other apps ignore it. It is never in a profile's folder, never sent to an index, never handed to your AI, whose access keys open profiles, not this folder. The device keeps a copy, so names show with nothing unlocked, and every unlock that opens your Profiles folder puts the entry's in its place.
- **Passkeys you add.** Each one after the first is what the standard calls a backup: it gives a seed of its own, whose Profiles folder holds yours in its [seed entry](https://github.com/foundationforest/standard/blob/main/folders/README.md#the-seed-entry), locked to that folder alone, as the standard says. Adding one writes a `backup/<id>` entry in your Profiles folder, holding its seed, locked the same way; then its own folder, its first write paid with a credit from your wallet. If that fails, the `backup/<id>` entry is deleted again, and one red line says the passkey wasn't added. Every way of adding one does the same: from Passkeys, to save one on this device, or from your 24 words. Every unlock follows a backup to your seed, one step and never more, so a passkey added or removed on another device counts at once; one that no longer opens the profiles this device lists (one removed since) stops, in one red line. Removing one deletes its seed entry, then its `backup/<id>` entry. The entry holds the seed and nothing else: where a passkey lives, from an id each new passkey sends naming its maker, and the name you give it stay on the device that added or renamed it.
- **The passkey belongs to app.forest.foundation,** so no other site can ask it for your seed, not even another page under forest.foundation.
- **Credits.** The host charges by the byte, a tiny fraction of a cent per listing or message, a little more for a photo. Each new profile gets a credit from your wallet, your Profiles folder's balance, and when one runs low the app tops it up. The host sees the wallet pay for each top-up but can't tell which profile it went to: it signed the credits blind.
- **Your AI's connection** is a folder like any other, holding its access keys instead of listings, named by a random key, so its name links to nothing. It holds the AI app's name and, for each profile you ticked, that profile's access key, all locked to the connector and the folder alone; the AI app's login carries a pointer to it, locked to the connector. Each profile ticked lists its key in its profile entry, with a note only your seed opens naming the folder, so any device with your seed finds your AI apps again: at Start, and on a device in use at its next unlock, once a profile it reads lists a key it doesn't know. That reads each of your profiles' folders and each AI app's folder their notes name, in the background; reading is free, and nothing is written. The folder's first write is paid from your wallet. Unticking a profile ends its key; Disconnect deletes the connection, then ends every key it held.
- **Avatars.** Your own profiles show their market's icon, wherever they show (Home, the sidebar, a profile's header, From, the ticks for an AI app), so you tell them apart by their markets. Anyone else's profile is an animal, one of forty, picked on your device from the profile's address, so the same profile is the same animal on every device and for everyone using this app; nothing is stored or published. It's this app's own drawing: another app, or the index, may draw a profile its own way (the index shows its market's icon). The icons come from the small table the index draws from too (services' `index/src/avatar.ts`), on tiles and the role sheet too; a market the table doesn't list shows its name's first letter. The animals are Microsoft's [Fluent Emoji](https://github.com/microsoft/fluentui-emoji), High Contrast, MIT licensed.
- **Type.** Figtree sets every word, titles at its heaviest. The name Forest, where the app writes it (Start, the setup panel, the sidebar), is drawn once from Fraunces, as the landing page sets it, so no other font is downloaded. Both under the SIL Open Font License.
- **Photos** are redrawn on your device before they're sent: a JPEG, no side longer than 1600 pixels, nothing but the pixels. So a photo's location, and the rest of what a camera writes into it, never leaves your device. A listing names each photo by its fingerprint (SHA-256); the host keeps it while a listing names it, and serves it to anyone.
- **Ratings** come from an index, ours here, which reads every host, counts verified profiles, and rates each one from the reviews it counts, weighing each by how much it trusts the reviewer. Each index rates its own way, so another may show another number. The app asks it, from your device, what it says of each of your profiles. A review's score is the reviewer's; the rating is the index's.
- **What the device keeps:** the id of the passkey in use and of each one it has made or used, your profiles' labels and addresses, when each profile was last used here, your Profiles folder's address and the label entries it has read there, how you're verified (worked out again from your Profiles folder's stamps at each unlock that opens it), your AI apps (each one's name, the day it connected, its folder's address and its profiles), the addresses of the access keys it knows of, where you left off in each inbox, the time of the newest review, vouch or report about each profile you've seen, where each passkey it made or added lives and the name you gave any, the names you gave others, the centre of each area you've put on the map (moved a little from where you put it), and your theme; while a verification is under way, its check and its session, the ID check's payment and how many tries it opened, and the buy for its gift; and an ID check's payment no stamp has used yet; and, for one tab, while a check takes it to Stripe or Didit and back, an AI app's login or a Share link waiting through Start and the verification. Never your seed, its 24 words, a key, a stamp or a message.
- **Stamps that count** are the ones from the verifier's current keys, as it lists them: one from a key it no longer signs with (its ID key before services#123) counts for nothing. The app shows no check for it, and no profile carries a verification of it.
- **Proofs** run in the background on your device, from files the app serves (about 7 MB, downloaded once).
- **Pay addresses.** Each profile lists its own, for when payments come; the app doesn't show them yet.
- **Same seed, same Forest.** Everything the app writes follows the standard and the reference app in services (`mcp/test/app.ts`) exactly, and the passkeys you add, which the reference app doesn't make, follow the standard's own example for backups, so any app with your seed sees the same profiles, stamps, connections and passkeys.

## Run your own

Run `./standard.sh`, which fetches the standard, the markets and services (for the host's credits client) at the commits in `STANDARD`, `MARKETS` and `SERVICES`; then `npm ci` and `npm run dev`. To test it, run `./standard.sh services` instead, which also readies services' own verifier, host, index and connector to run on your machine, as services' end-to-end test runs them. Then `npm run check` checks the types, `npm test` the flows against those services, and `npm run test:browser` the whole app in Chromium, with a virtual passkey, against them too. Node 22.18 or later.

When services moves its `MARKETS`, the app follows by itself within a day: `MARKETS` to services' and `SERVICES` to services' main, once every check here passes with them ([`markets.yml`](.github/workflows/markets.yml)); `STANDARD` moves by hand, here and in services. To move them now, Run workflow on [its page](https://github.com/foundationforest/app/actions/workflows/markets.yml).

To host a copy, serve the build from any static host with the security headers in `vercel.json`, and set the services it talks to in its one config file. A copy at another address makes its own passkeys, so its own seeds.

## How we run it

On Vercel, at app.forest.foundation. The page talks only to our host, verifier, connector and index, and, on the place screen, OpenFreeMap, for the map; it shows photos from our host's bucket: a strict content security policy lets it reach nothing else. The page loads from the network first, so a new version never leaves you on an old one; a page left open when a new version goes live says so, in one quiet line at the top, with Reload. No analytics and no logs of our own; Vercel keeps its own edge logs.

## Promises

- **It never stores your seed on a device,** nor its 24 words, nor anything secret made from it. The words show only after an unlock, while their screen is open. After an unlock, it holds the seed in the page's memory 5 minutes since the last action, or until the page closes. Each passkey you add keeps a copy on the host, locked so only that passkey opens it.
- **It moves no money,** and keeps no key of yours past one action.
- **Nothing it writes lets a reader link two of your profiles.**
- **It writes only what the standard and the reference app write,** so any app with your seed finds what this one made.
- **When something can't be done, it says why, in one line.**

## Limits

- The app connects only to our connector, mcp.forest.foundation, for now.
- No push notifications: what's new shows when the app is open.
- While the seed is held, the page's own code reads it without asking; what keeps any other code out is the content security policy.
- Hiding the page doesn't drop the seed: a locked phone or a covered window keeps it, so someone who unlocks your device while it's held can act as you without the passkey asking.
- The host you write to can link your profiles and your passkeys: one device writes them all, one wallet funds them, one device reads all their inboxes. It promises to keep no network addresses in its logs; that's its promise, not the app's.
- To show your ratings, the app asks the index about each of your profiles from your device, so the index can link them, as the host already can; opening someone's profile asks it about theirs, so it sees whose profiles you look at.
- What's new goes by the time each writer puts on what they write: a review dated in the past shows no dot.
- A web app's code comes from its server each time you open it, so you trust whoever serves it. It's open source, and you can run your own.
- Whoever holds any one of your passkeys holds the seed: each is as safe as the manager or key that keeps it. So does whoever reads your 24 words.
- Your first passkey can't be removed: it is your seed.
- Removing a passkey takes back nothing it already opened: whoever opened its seed entry has the seed, and so does anyone who opens a copy a host still keeps.
- Where a passkey lives shows only on the device that added it, and only when the passkey says which it is; a name you give it shows only on the device you named it on; elsewhere it shows as Passkey.
- Anyone can see that your Profiles folder holds names you gave, and about how many from the entry's size, though not whose or what they are.
- On a device that knows a Forest passkey, Start never asks First time here?, so never makes a passkey, even when that one was deleted from your manager; Type my 24 words, under No passkey found, still makes one, and clearing this site's data in the browser brings the question back.
- One host, ours, for everything you make, and only profiles on it can be messaged from here.
- Anyone can see when one of your profiles keeps a copy of what it sent, though not what it says or whom it's to; someone watching the other person's inbox could match the times.
- An area's moved centre is kept on the device you put it on: another device of yours moves the same place its own way.
- The map's tiles come from OpenFreeMap, which, like the network in front of it, sees the area you look at.
- Photos show as the host serves them; the app doesn't check them against their names yet.
- Not yet: payments between people, carrying your reputation from one profile to another, moving to another host.
- A proof takes a few seconds on a phone.

## FAQ

**What if I lose my passkey?**
Start on any device with another of your passkeys: every one opens the same profiles. Or, when Start finds no passkey, tap Type my 24 words, and the app makes a new passkey for them. Add one in You's Passkeys, in another password manager, on a second phone or on a security key, and remove a lost one there. Lose every passkey and your 24 words, and nobody can open your profiles again, not even the foundation.

**Is it free?**
The app and the face check are; the ID check is $5, one time, held on your card and charged only if it passes. The host charges for what you write, a tiny fraction of a cent per listing or message, and your verification comes with a gift that covers thousands of them. Buying more comes later.

**Why do I unlock to post, but not to look?**
Posting needs your seed, to sign as your profile, and your passkey gives it only after you unlock. The app then holds it a few minutes, so posting again soon asks nothing. Looking needs nothing: everything your profiles show is public, and your device remembers which ones are yours. Messages are the one thing locked to you, so opening them takes an unlock, unless the seed is held.

**Who sees my face?**
Didit, the provider our verifier uses, and for the ID check, your document too. The verifier reads the result, turns your selfie into numbers that go into your stamp, keeps none of it, and deletes the check at Didit an hour later; Didit keeps a template of your face so the same face can't be stamped twice. The app never sees any of it, and your profiles show only how you're verified. The details: [services/verifier](https://github.com/foundationforest/services/blob/main/verifier/README.md).
