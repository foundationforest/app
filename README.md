# app

The Forest app is your control panel for Forest: where you create your profiles, get verified, post, keep up with your feedback and messages, and connect your AI. Your profiles and everything they post live on hosts, open for anyone to read; the app reads them and writes for you. Your device keeps addresses and settings, never your seed, its 24 words, a key, a stamp or a message: the full list is under Behind the scenes.

Your seed is what all your profiles come from. It's kept by your passkey, in the password manager you choose. To act, you unlock: the app asks your passkey for the seed, and you allow it with your device's unlock (face, fingerprint or PIN). A button that will ask shows the unlock icon, a fingerprint, before its word, but on Start and its sheets, where every button asks.

The app is open source, so anyone can run one. The Forest Foundation runs this one, at https://app.forest.foundation. It's a web app for now; phone apps may come later, from the same code.

## How it works

Three places: **Home**, **Notifications** and **You**. On a phone they are three tabs at the bottom; on a laptop, a sidebar, with Messages and your profiles too: Profiles, with a small round plus beside it for a new one, then your profiles in Home's order, eight at most, then All profiles, which opens Home. On a laptop every screen is one column, centred, but Home's cards, which fill the width, and Messages, in two panes.

1. **Start.** One button, Start. On a device that knows your passkey, Start asks for it and takes you in: to Home, or to where setup was left. Once a device has used a passkey, Start asks for that one alone. On a new device, Start asks First time here? Yes, I'm new makes your passkey (your password manager keeps it, and with it your seed), then Get verified. I use Forest on another device asks for one you have: one synced here, or one on another device, such as your phone, through the QR code your browser shows. Every passkey you've added opens the same profiles. Let in by a passkey on another device, the app offers once to save one on this device too, and from then on this device asks that one. When no passkey answers, No passkey found offers Try again, or Type my 24 words: type the 24 words You shows, in order, and the app makes a new passkey that opens your seed from then on, as one added in You does, and you're in. Continue waits for all 24; words that can't be used say why in one line before any passkey is made; words that open a different Forest from the one this device lists are refused, and Forget this device comes first, so one device never mixes two seeds.

2. **Get verified.** The first time, a verifier checks that you're a real person it hasn't seen before, and signs a stamp to your seed, so people know you're real. Two cards, each one tap:
   - **Selfie, free.** A quick selfie, opened at once.
   - **ID, $5.** Your ID and a selfie. Indexes can count it as stronger, it comes with ten times the credits, and it helps fund the foundation. Its sheet shows its price, its three tries, and what the check keeps and who keeps it, word for word as the verifier states it, before Pay. You pay the verifier directly, through Stripe; the app never sees your card.

   Both run with Didit, the identity-check company our verifier uses, in Didit's own tab; meanwhile the app waits, and moves on by itself when the stamp comes. If the check takes longer than the app holds your seed, you unlock once more to keep it. With the stamp comes a gift of credits, which pay the host for what you write; the cards name credits only where the verifier gives them. Both go into your Profiles folder, a private folder only your seed opens, so any device you start on finds them. A check that doesn't pass says so in one line on Get verified, and the next tap opens a new one. If Didit has seen your face before, a person at the foundation reviews it: Get verified says so, and the app keeps checking; close Forest, and Start picks up there. Going back loses nothing: pick the same check again and it goes on where it was; switch, and a payment you made waits for the ID check. Close the page midway, and Start picks up where you were. You can add the other check later, in You.

   Then your first profile: every market as a tile; tap one, then your role (I'm a tutor, I'm a student), and the app makes it, or Later. Then Connect your AI (7), or Skip.

3. **Home.** Your profiles. Beside the title, how you're verified, once, since every profile of yours carries the same checks: a light badge with a face for the selfie check, a solid one with an ID card for the ID check, each saying so when tapped; a check you haven't done shows as its badge's outline, dashed and muted, and a tap starts it (the ID's opens its sheet). Then one card per profile, with its avatar (its market's icon on its market's color, solid when it offers, light when it requests), its market in bold and its role under it, its rating and how many reviews it has, and how many things are new on it (messages, reviews, vouches and reports). Profiles with something new come first (a message, a review, a vouch or a report, or a listing to update), then the ones you used most recently on this device (opened, posted from, written from). On a laptop, the cards fill the width, as many columns of about 300px as fit, each row one height, a name too long ending short. Past eight profiles, a search box finds yours by name. A profile's own page keeps its badge in its header, and other people's profiles show theirs everywhere. At the top, Search Forest opens the index. To make a new profile, pick a market and a role and unlock: the app makes it, proving it comes from a verified seed without saying which. Share hands out a profile's page on the index, for anyone who wants to message or review it. Profiles made on another device show here after one unlock.

4. **Listings.** Post an offer or a request from a profile, as its role posts. The form is the market's own file, read as it is: a question that depends on earlier answers shows once they hold, and goes when a changed answer means it no longer applies, so clothing asks who it's for and the kind, then one size question. A listing can have photos, a price (and what it's per, when the market says, such as per hour), the market's choices, several where it allows, a long list from a sheet with a search, a place and hours, each on a screen of its own. A choice that takes one value looks like one: side by side, or chips where picking one unpicks the other, or on its sheet rows with a radio mark, a tap picking it and closing the sheet; one that takes several shows a tick on each picked, and its sheet tick boxes and Done. The market's file says which, for every market alike; a listing can carry a contact. A place is set on a map, on a screen of its own: an area or an exact spot. An area is a circle you tap or drag into place and size, from the market's own size up to 50 km. Its centre is moved a few hundred metres from where you put it, once, and kept on the device, and an area put within 800 metres of a centre already kept takes that one, so your listings never average out to your home; the spot you put it on stays inside the circle, which glides to where it will be posted. An exact spot is a pin you drop, posted exactly there, for anyone to see. Your location only centres the map: the app reads it for a new place where your browser already allows it, or when you tap the map's own button. A contact (a phone, a handle, an email) is public, and the same contact on two listings links them. You can edit or delete a listing. When the market changes and a listing no longer fits it, its card says it needs an update, and so does Notifications, and it opens with what still fits filled in.

5. **Notifications.** Every review, vouch and report about any of your profiles, under Today and Earlier, newest first, in full, each with the avatar of who wrote it, with nothing unlocked; the new ones are marked until you've seen them, and a tap opens that profile's Reviews. And a notice for each of your own listings and reviews a market outgrew (it needs an update: the market changed), which opens it with what still fits filled in; posted, the notice goes. Its tab counts both, and new messages too; at its top, Messages, with how many are new. Each profile's page shows, under its name, the rating an index gives it (ours here) and how many reviews, or with no score yet what came (1 vouch); a tap opens its Reviews: the score large, how many, which index rated it, and what that index counted about it, newest first, each From who wrote it and opening in full; "No reviews yet" when nothing came. Then two tabs: its offers or requests, each a card with its price, and Written by you: the reviews, vouches and reports the profile wrote, each About who it's about, which you can edit or delete. You can start a review from Written by you, a thread, or a review you received; from a profile, it first asks who: someone the profile has a conversation with, or their link or address. Then one of three: a score from 1 to 10, in tenths, with the market's own questions; a vouch, that they're real and honest, without a score; or a report, that they're fake or a scam. None names which of their listings it's about. Each is about someone on the other side of the same market (in a market with one role, like tennis, the same role), never one of your own profiles, and the app checks that before you unlock, in one line. Written again about the same person, a review, vouch or report goes in place of the one before: an index counts one of each.

6. **Messages.** Messages, from the round button at the top of Notifications (on a laptop, the sidebar), hold every profile's conversations, newest first, with a chip for each of your profiles to show its own; the same button at a profile's top opens them on its chip. They show how many are new with nothing unlocked, and on which of your profiles: who wrote one shows only once it's opened. Opening them takes one unlock, unless your seed is held, and then they open by themselves as they come. Each thread shows who it's with (their market and role, and how they're verified) and to which of your profiles; reply there, or review them. On a laptop, Messages is two panes: the conversations on the left, with their chips, and the one open on the right; a phone shows one at a time. A new message, from the pencil at the top, goes to an address or a profile's page on the index, one of your own profiles too, from your profile on the other side of their market (in a market with one role, like tennis, the same role), or a new one when you have none. A message is locked so only they can read it, and paid in tiny amounts from your profile's credits. Every message a profile sends, from the app or your AI, keeps a locked copy in its folder, so on any device the app shows the whole conversation, and your AI everything since you connected it.

7. **Your AI.** To connect it, add a connector in your AI app with the address https://mcp.forest.foundation (Connect your AI, right after your first profile and in You's Your AI, shows how: copy the address, add it in Claude or ChatGPT as a connector, sign in when it asks), and if it asks how to sign in, choose to sign in now: Claude offers No sign-in first, and with it Claude can read but never act. The first time your AI acts, it sends you to the app's connect page, which shows the AI app's name and, under it, the site it sends you back to: that site is what says which app it really is. Tick the profiles it may act for, unlock once, and you're back in your chat. If this browser isn't in yet, Start comes first, and, if you're new, getting verified and your first profile; then the same page. Each profile ticked hands it an access key that can read, post, read the inbox and message; the AI never gets your seed, a main key or your addresses, and the app never shows the key or keeps it. Your AI app's own settings choose which of its tools it uses.

8. **You.** One column, on a phone and a laptop alike. At the top, how you're verified: Selfie and ID, each Verified, or for one you haven't done what it gives (the ID: stronger trust, and ten times the free credits) and Add, which opens it. Then a row for each of these, opening its own screen:
   - **Passkeys:** each passkey, by its password manager's name or the day it was added, This device under one this device made, Backup under any other added after the first. Add one, with one unlock, in another password manager, on a second phone or on a security key (the passkeys this device knows are excluded, so your password manager doesn't keep a second one); or remove one. Under If you lose every passkey, Your 24 words: a sheet warns that anyone who sees them can act as you, then Show words asks your passkey, even when the seed is held, and shows your seed as 24 words, with one line: never type them into a site or app you don't trust. Nothing stores them; they show only while that screen is open.
   - **Your AI:** your AI apps, each with how many profiles it acts for and since when; each opens to change which profiles it acts for, keeping at least one, or Disconnect it: it stops at once, and what it wrote stays. Connect another, or, when you connected one from another browser, Find AI apps finds it here.
   - **Appearance:** Auto (your device's), Light or Dark, on a sheet.
   - **About Forest:** Privacy, Terms and the source code, each in a new tab, and the version.

   At the foot, Forget this device (your passkey stays in your manager).

## Behind the scenes

- **Your seed** is your first passkey's PRF secret: a value the passkey gives only after you unlock, the same 32 bytes on every device it syncs to. After an unlock, the app holds it in the page's memory 5 minutes since the last action, or until the page closes, or until Forget. What the page does by itself, opening messages again as new ones come, uses the seed held but doesn't count as an action. A page only hidden (another window over it, another tab, the phone locked) keeps it, and an action already running finishes first. It is never stored on a device, nor anything secret made from it. Each passkey you add keeps a copy on the host, locked so only that passkey opens it.
- **Your 24 words** are your seed itself, as the standard spells it (`exportWords`): BIP39's words for its 32 bytes, without BIP39's passphrase step. Passkeys shows them after a fresh unlock, even when the seed is held, and only while their screen is open; they live in the page's memory while it is, never its history, the seed they came from is dropped at once, and a reload there opens Passkeys. Type my 24 words, under Start's No passkey found, reads them back (`importWords`) and checks, before any passkey is made: all 24, each in its place; verified, a stamp in their Profiles folder, whose wallet pays for the new passkey's folder; and the Forest this device lists, if it lists one. Words that spell an added passkey's own seed are followed one step, to the seed it opens. Then the app makes a passkey and adds it as below, so from then on it opens your seed.
- **Passkeys you add.** Each one after the first is what the standard calls a backup: it gives a seed of its own, whose Profiles folder holds yours in its [seed entry](https://github.com/foundationforest/standard/blob/main/folders/README.md#the-seed-entry), locked to that folder alone, as the standard says. Adding one writes a `backup/<id>` entry in your Profiles folder, holding its seed, locked the same way; then its own folder, its first write paid with a credit from your wallet. If that fails, the `backup/<id>` entry is deleted again, and one red line says the passkey wasn't added. Every way of adding one does the same: from Passkeys, to save one on this device, or from your 24 words. Every unlock follows a backup to your seed, one step and never more, so a passkey added or removed on another device counts at once; one that no longer opens the profiles this device lists (one removed since) stops, in one red line. Removing one deletes its seed entry, then its `backup/<id>` entry. The entry holds the seed and nothing else: the day a passkey was added is the entry's own time, and the name of its password manager, from an id each new passkey sends naming it, stays on the device that added it, with whether that device made it for itself (This device).
- **The passkey belongs to app.forest.foundation,** so no other site can ask it for your seed, not even another page under forest.foundation.
- **Credits.** The host charges by the byte, a tiny fraction of a cent per listing or message, a little more for a photo. Each new profile gets a credit from your wallet, your Profiles folder's balance, and when one runs low the app tops it up. The host sees the wallet pay for each top-up but can't tell which profile it went to: it signed the credits blind.
- **Your AI's connection** is a folder like any other, holding its access keys instead of listings, named by a random key, so its name links to nothing. It holds the AI app's name and, for each profile you ticked, that profile's access key, all locked to the connector and the folder alone; the AI app's login carries a pointer to it, locked to the connector. Each profile ticked lists its key in its profile entry, with a note only your seed opens naming the folder, so any device with your seed finds your AI apps again. The folder's first write is paid from your wallet. Unticking a profile ends its key; Disconnect deletes the connection, then ends every key it held.
- **Photos** are redrawn on your device before they're sent: a JPEG, no side longer than 1600 pixels, nothing but the pixels. So a photo's location, and the rest of what a camera writes into it, never leaves your device. A listing names each photo by its fingerprint (SHA-256); the host keeps it while a listing names it, and serves it to anyone.
- **Ratings** come from an index, ours here, which reads every host, counts verified profiles, and rates each one from the reviews it counts, weighing each by how much it trusts the reviewer. Each index rates its own way, so another may show another number. The app asks it, from your device, what it says of each of your profiles. A review's score is the reviewer's; the rating is the index's.
- **What the device keeps:** the id of the passkey in use and of each one it has made or used, your profiles' labels and addresses, when each profile was last used here, your Profiles folder's address and the label entries it has read there, how you're verified, your AI apps (each one's name, the day it connected, its folder's address and its profiles), the addresses of the access keys it knows of, where you left off in each inbox, the time of the newest review, vouch or report about each profile you've seen, the password manager's name of each passkey it made or added and which it made for itself, the centre of each area you've put on the map (moved a little from where you put it), and your theme; while a verification is under way, its check and its session, the ID check's payment, and the buy for its gift; and an ID check's payment no stamp has used yet. Never your seed, its 24 words, a key, a stamp or a message.
- **Proofs** run in the background on your device, from files the app serves (about 7 MB, downloaded once).
- **Pay addresses.** Each profile lists its own, for when payments come; the app doesn't show them yet.
- **Same seed, same Forest.** Everything the app writes follows the standard and the reference app in services (`mcp/test/app.ts`) exactly, and the passkeys you add, which the reference app doesn't make, follow the standard's own example for backups, so any app with your seed sees the same profiles, stamps, connections and passkeys.

## Run your own

Run `./standard.sh`, which fetches the standard, the markets and services (for the host's credits client) at the commits in `STANDARD`, `MARKETS` and `SERVICES`; then `npm ci` and `npm run dev`. To test it, run `./standard.sh services` instead, which also readies services' own verifier, host, index and connector to run on your machine, as services' end-to-end test runs them. Then `npm run check` checks the types, `npm test` the flows against those services, and `npm run test:browser` the whole app in Chromium, with a virtual passkey, against them too. Node 22.18 or later.

When services moves its `MARKETS`, the app follows by itself within a day: `MARKETS` to services' and `SERVICES` to services' main, once every check here passes with them ([`markets.yml`](.github/workflows/markets.yml)); `STANDARD` moves by hand, here and in services. To move them now, Run workflow on [its page](https://github.com/foundationforest/app/actions/workflows/markets.yml).

To host a copy, serve the build from any static host with the security headers in `vercel.json`, and set the services it talks to in its one config file. A copy at another address makes its own passkeys, so its own seeds.

## How we run it

On Vercel, at app.forest.foundation. The page talks only to our host, verifier, connector and index, and, on the place screen, OpenFreeMap, for the map; it shows photos from our host's bucket: a strict content security policy lets it reach nothing else. The page loads from the network first, so a new version never leaves you on an old one; a page left open when a new version goes live says so, in one quiet line at the top, with Reload. No analytics and no logs of our own; Vercel keeps its own edge logs.

## Promises

- **It never stores your seed on a device,** nor its 24 words, nor anything secret made from it. The words show only after an unlock, while their screen is open. After an unlock, it holds the seed in the page's memory 5 minutes since the last action, or until the page closes. Each passkey you add keeps a copy on the host, locked so only that passkey opens it.
- **It moves no money,** and keeps no key of yours past one action.
- **Nothing it writes lets a reader link two of your profiles.**
- **It writes only what the standard and the reference app write,** so any app with your seed finds what this one made.
- **When something can't be done, it says why, in one line.**

## Limits

- The app connects only to our connector, mcp.forest.foundation, for now.
- No push notifications: what's new shows when the app is open.
- While the seed is held, the page's own code reads it without asking; what keeps any other code out is the content security policy.
- Hiding the page doesn't drop the seed: a locked phone or a covered window keeps it, so someone who unlocks your device while it's held can act as you without the passkey asking.
- The host you write to can link your profiles and your passkeys: one device writes them all, one wallet funds them, one device reads all their inboxes. It promises to keep no network addresses in its logs; that's its promise, not the app's.
- To show your ratings, the app asks the index about each of your profiles from your device, so the index can link them, as the host already can.
- What's new goes by the time each writer puts on what they write: a review dated in the past shows no dot.
- A web app's code comes from its server each time you open it, so you trust whoever serves it. It's open source, and you can run your own.
- Whoever holds any one of your passkeys holds the seed: each is as safe as the manager or key that keeps it. So does whoever reads your 24 words.
- Your first passkey can't be removed: it is your seed.
- Removing a passkey takes back nothing it already opened: whoever opened its seed entry has the seed, and so does anyone who opens a copy a host still keeps.
- A passkey's password manager shows by name only on the device that added it, and only when the passkey says which it is; elsewhere it shows by the day it was added, and your first passkey as Your first passkey. This device shows only on the device that made the passkey, so a passkey synced to another device shows there without it.
- On a device that knows a Forest passkey, Start never asks First time here?, so never makes a passkey, even when that one was deleted from your manager; Type my 24 words, under No passkey found, still makes one, and clearing this site's data in the browser brings the question back.
- One host, ours, for everything you make, and only profiles on it can be messaged from here.
- Anyone can see when one of your profiles keeps a copy of what it sent, though not what it says or whom it's to; someone watching the other person's inbox could match the times.
- An area's moved centre is kept on the device you put it on: another device of yours moves the same place its own way.
- The map's tiles come from OpenFreeMap, which, like the network in front of it, sees the area you look at.
- Photos show as the host serves them; the app doesn't check them against their names yet.
- Not yet: payments between people, carrying your reputation from one profile to another, moving to another host.
- A proof takes a few seconds on a phone.

## FAQ

**What if I lose my passkey?**
Start on any device with another of your passkeys: every one opens the same profiles. Or, when Start finds no passkey, tap Type my 24 words, and the app makes a new passkey for them. Add one in You's Passkeys, in another password manager, on a second phone or on a security key, and remove a lost one there. Lose every passkey and your 24 words, and nobody can open your profiles again, not even the foundation.

**Is it free?**
The app and the face check are; the ID check is $5. The host charges for what you write, a tiny fraction of a cent per listing or message, and your verification comes with a gift that covers thousands of them. Buying more comes later.

**Why do I unlock to post, but not to look?**
Posting needs your seed, to sign as your profile, and your passkey gives it only after you unlock. The app then holds it a few minutes, so posting again soon asks nothing. Looking needs nothing: everything your profiles show is public, and your device remembers which ones are yours. Messages are the one thing locked to you, so opening them takes an unlock, unless the seed is held.

**Who sees my face?**
Didit, the provider our verifier uses, and for the ID check, your document too. The verifier reads the result, turns your selfie into numbers that go into your stamp, keeps none of it, and deletes the check at Didit an hour later; Didit keeps a template of your face so the same face can't be stamped twice. The app never sees any of it, and your profiles show only how you're verified. The details: [services/verifier](https://github.com/foundationforest/services/blob/main/verifier/README.md).

**Stuck, or paid and still waiting?**
Write to support@forest.foundation.
