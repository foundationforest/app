# app

The Forest app is your control panel for Forest: where you create your profiles, get verified, post, keep up with your reviews and messages, and connect your AI. It stores nothing of yours. Your profiles and everything they post live on hosts, open for anyone to read; the app reads them and writes for you.

It never stores your seed on a device either, the secret all your profiles come from. Your first passkey keeps it, in your password manager, and every passkey you add opens it too: each one added keeps a copy on the host, locked so only that passkey opens it. When you do something, the app asks a passkey for the seed, and you allow it with your device's unlock (face, fingerprint or PIN). After that unlock, the app holds the seed in the page's memory 5 minutes since the last action, or until the page closes, so what you do next asks nothing. A page that is only hidden (another window over it, another tab, the phone locked) keeps it. A button shows the unlock icon only when tapping it will ask.

The app is open source, so anyone can run one. The Forest Foundation runs this one, at https://app.forest.foundation. It's a web app for now; phone apps may come later, from the same code.

## How it works

Three places: your **Profiles**, your **Notifications** and **Settings**.

1. **Start.** On a device that has used your Forest passkey, one button, Start: it asks for that passkey alone, you unlock, and you're in. On a device that hasn't, two: Create a passkey makes one, kept by your password manager, its secret your seed; I already have one opens the passkey sheet, which finds yours if it synced here, or, through its QR code, lets a device that doesn't sync with this one answer. On a device that knows a passkey, Start never offers to make another. If the sheet closes with no answer, the app says only that no passkey was chosen: a browser can't tell a closed sheet from no passkey. Every passkey you added opens the same profiles: Start finds the seed it opens and goes on with that one. Right after Create a passkey, one question, New to Forest?: Yes, I'm new goes on to Get verified; I already use Forest, for a passkey made by mistake, asks for the passkey you use, adds the new one to it, and takes you straight to your profiles. It asks again whenever Start opens a passkey that isn't verified and has no check under way, so a reload doesn't lose it. Let in by a passkey on another device, a phone through the sheet's QR code (the browser says the passkey isn't on this device), the app asks once, Save a passkey on this device?: Save makes one here, adds it, and from then this device asks that one; Not now goes on.

2. **Get verified.** The first time, a verifier checks that you're a real person it hasn't seen before, and signs a stamp to your seed. Two ways:
   - **Face, free.** A selfie.
   - **ID, $5.** Your ID and a selfie. Indexes can count it as stronger, it comes with ten times the credits, and it helps fund the foundation. Before you pay, the app shows its price and its three tries, and, a tap away, what the check keeps and who keeps it, word for word as the verifier states it. You pay the verifier directly, through Stripe; the app never sees your card.

   Ours runs both with Didit, in their own tab. The app holds the seed meanwhile, so a check done within 5 minutes of your last action is kept with nothing asked; a longer one asks once, at Continue. With the stamp comes a gift of credits, which pay the host for what you write. Both go into your Profiles folder, a private folder only your seed opens, so any device you start on finds them. If Didit has seen your face before, a person at the foundation reviews it, and the app keeps checking. Every step has a back, to the step before (Get verified's, to New to Forest?, or to Start once a check is under way), and back loses nothing: pick the same check again and it goes on where it was; switch, and a payment you made waits for the ID check. Close the page midway, and Start picks up where you were. Then you make your first profile, and right after it, once, Add a second passkey: "Add a second, so losing one doesn't lose your profiles.", with Add and Not now.

3. **Profiles.** Your home: one card per profile, those that offer first. At the top right, the inbox, always there: every profile's messages, with how many are new once any is. Each shows its market and role, the market in bold and the role quieter (**Clothing** seller), the shield of its strongest check, a dot when something is new on it (a message, or a review, vouch or report), and its offers or requests, its rating and how many reviews it has. The shield is an outline for the face check and filled for the ID check, the same wherever a profile shows (cards, a profile, threads, To and From, a review's From, the connect page, Settings); tapped, it says "Verified by face" or "Verified with ID". New profile is two steps: the markets, to search, then a market's roles; one you have says Yours and opens its profile; any other shows a +: tap it and unlock, and the app makes it, proving it comes from a verified seed without saying which. At a profile's top right, Share hands out its page on the index (https://index.forest.foundation/profiles/ and its address) through your phone's share sheet, or copies it where there is none, for anyone who wants to message or review it; beside it, the inbox, with how many of that profile's messages are new, opens its conversations. Under its name, one line: its rating and how many reviews ("★ 9.5 · 1 review"), which opens its Reviews, with "· 1 new" when something is. A profile made on another device shows as one row at the top, New profiles from another device; one unlock reads them, and their cards show. Nothing else here needs you to unlock: your device remembers which profiles are yours, and everything they show is public.

4. **Posting.** Posting starts from a profile: New offer, or New request, as its role posts, at its bottom beside Review. The form is the market's own file, read as it is: the fields that matter most first, then the rest; a question that depends on earlier answers shows right after them once they hold, and goes when a changed answer means it no longer applies, so clothing asks who it's for and the kind, then one size question. Photos, a price (and what it's per, when the market says, such as per hour), choices, several where the market allows, each by its label where the market gives one (XL, TV), a place, and a contact on every post. A price reads as people write it, in your device's number format: $20, €30 per hour. On a post's row it is the second line, in every market. A place is a point and how far round it reaches: one line, "Within 5 km of you", with the area's name if you give one; a slider, from the market's own distance up to 50 km, and 0, exactly where you are, only when you slide there. Its point is your device's location, moved a few hundred metres once and kept on the device, so your posts never average out to your home, and your real spot stays inside the circle. The app reads your location at once where your browser already allows it; otherwise it asks only when you tap Use my location, and without it, one line says why the post needs it. A contact (a phone, a handle, an email) is public, and the same contact on two posts links them. Post and unlock. Tap a post to edit it; delete is there too. A post that no longer fits its market, since the market changed, says Update this post on its row; open it, and what still fits is filled in.

5. **Reviews.** A profile's rating line opens its Reviews: the rating an index gives it, ours here, what that index counted about it, and what it wrote about others. A new review, vouch or report about a profile shows in Notifications, counted on its bell, as its card's dot and as "1 new" on its rating line; Notifications and Reviews mark the new ones, and once either has shown them they're new no more. Reviewing starts from a profile's Review, Reviews, a thread or a review you received, with who it's about shown as soon as you give their address. A review gives a score from 1 to 10, in tenths (the slider shows none until you touch it), and may name which of their posts it's about; no score makes it a vouch ("I know they're real and honest"). A report ("this is fake or a scam") is its own action, in red. Each is about someone on the other side of the same market, and the app checks that before you unlock. For now, for testing, it may be another of your own profiles; that may close before real users.

6. **Notifications.** Every review, vouch and report about any of your profiles, with nothing unlocked, newest first, in full, a dot on the new ones; tap one to open it. The bell counts the new ones. Messages aren't here: they're the inbox's, at the top right, as on Profiles.

7. **Messages.** The inbox at the top right of Profiles and Notifications opens every profile's conversations, newest first, a dot on the new ones; the one at a profile's top, that profile's. With the seed held, they open by themselves, and again as new ones come. Otherwise one row says how many are new ("3 new messages", or "No new messages"), with Open: one unlock, and then every thread opens, each showing who it's from (their market, their role, and the shield when they're verified). In a thread each message shows when it was sent, with the day wherever it changes; reply in one row, the text box and a round Send, or review them. New message, at the bottom, opens a new message, all of it there at once: To, an address or a profile's page on the index, with who it is under it, one of your own profiles too; From, your profile on the other side, or New profile when you have none; the message, and Send. A message is locked so only they can read it, and paid in tiny amounts from your profile's credits; a copy of what you sent stays in your profile's own folder, locked to you, so every device you start on shows it.

8. **Your AI.** Connect an AI, from Settings, is three steps: in your AI app, add a connector, and if it asks how to sign in, pick Sign in now (Claude offers No sign-in first, and with it Claude never logs in, so it can read but never act); paste this address, https://mcp.forest.foundation, with Copy; it brings you back here to pick its profiles. Right after your first profile is made, and Add a second passkey, one screen, Use Forest from your AI, offers it once. The first time your AI acts, it sends you here, to the connect page: the AI app's name, big, and under it the site it sends you back to, which is what says which app it really is; your profiles to tick, none ticked unless you have just one; Connect, one unlock, and you're back in your chat. Each profile ticked hands it an access key that can read, post, read the inbox and message; the AI never gets your seed or a main key, and the app never shows the key or keeps it. Your AI app's own settings choose which of its tools it uses. With nothing set up here yet, Start, the verification and your first profile run first, then the same page; there Start offers I already have one first, since the login often opens in a browser that has never seen this app.

9. **Settings.** How you're verified: Face and ID, each with its shield, or the ID check, + $5, if you only have the face; your passkeys, every one alike: Your first passkey, then each one added, by its password manager's name where this device knows it, else the day it was added, each with Remove, tapped twice; with only one, a quiet line, "Add a second, so losing one doesn't lose your profiles."; then + Add a passkey: one unlock, then a new passkey, named Forest; the passkeys this device knows are excluded, so your password manager doesn't keep a second one: save it in another password manager, on a second phone, or on a security key. None of the list needs an unlock; Remove takes one; your AI apps, each with the day it connected and how many profiles it acts for: open one to tick or untick its profiles, at least one staying ticked, or Disconnect it (it stops at once, and what it wrote stays); + Connect an AI; and Find AI apps when a profile lists an access key this device doesn't know, as after connecting from another browser; the device's look, light or dark; Forget this device (your passkey stays in your manager).

## Behind the scenes

- **Your seed** is your first passkey's PRF secret: a value the passkey gives only after you unlock, the same 32 bytes on every device it syncs to. After an unlock, the app holds it in the page's memory 5 minutes since the last action, or until the page closes, or until Forget; a page only hidden keeps it, and an action already running finishes first. What the page does by itself, opening messages again as new ones come, uses the seed held and never restarts the 5 minutes. It is never stored on a device, nor anything secret made from it; the device keeps addresses. Each passkey you add keeps a copy on the host, locked so only that passkey opens it.
- **Passkeys you add.** The screens call every passkey alike; underneath, each one after the first is what the standard calls a backup. Its secret is a seed of its own, whose Profiles folder holds yours in its seed entry, locked to that folder alone, as the standard's seed entry says. Every unlock follows it, one step and never more, so a passkey added or removed on another device counts at once; a passkey that no longer opens the profiles this device lists (one removed since) stops, in one red line. Adding one writes a `backup/<id>` entry in your Profiles folder, holding its seed, locked to that folder alone; then its own folder, its first write a credit from your wallet. If that fails, the `backup/<id>` entry is deleted again, and one red line says the passkey wasn't added. New to Forest?'s I already use Forest writes the same for the passkey just made, and Save a passkey on this device? for the one it makes. Remove deletes its seed entry, then its `backup/<id>` entry. The day one was added is its entry's own time. The entry holds the seed and nothing else, so its password manager's name, from the AAGUID a new passkey sends, stays on the device that added it.
- **The passkey belongs to app.forest.foundation,** so no other site can ask it for your seed, not even another page under forest.foundation.
- **Credits.** The host charges by the byte, a tiny fraction of a cent per post or message, a little more for a photo. Each new profile gets a credit from your wallet, your Profiles folder's balance, and when one runs low the app tops it up. The host sees the wallet pay for each top-up but can't tell which profile it went to: it signed the credits blind.
- **Your AI's connection** is a folder of its own on the host, named by a random key, so its name links to nothing. It holds the AI app's name and, for each profile you ticked, that profile's access key, all locked to the connector and the folder alone; the AI app's login carries a pointer to it, locked to the connector. Each profile ticked lists its key in its profile entry, with a note only your seed opens naming the folder, so any device with your seed finds your AI apps again. The folder's first write is paid from your wallet. Unticking a profile ends its key; Disconnect deletes the connection, then ends every key it held. All of it in the connector's own format, as the reference app writes it.
- **Photos** are redrawn on your device before they're sent: a JPEG, no side longer than 1600 pixels, nothing but the pixels. So a photo's location, and the rest of what a camera writes into it, never leaves your device. A post names each photo by its fingerprint (SHA-256); the host keeps it while a post names it, and serves it to anyone.
- **Ratings** come from an index, which reads every host, counts verified profiles, and rates each one from the reviews it counts. The app asks it, from your device, what it says of each of your profiles. A review's score is the reviewer's; the rating is the index's.
- **What the device keeps:** your passkey's id, your profiles' labels and addresses, your Profiles folder's address and the label entries it has read there, how you're verified, your AI apps (each one's name, the day it connected, its folder's address and its profiles), the addresses of the access keys it knows of, where you left off in each inbox, the time of the newest review about each profile you've seen, the password manager's name of each passkey it added, the point for each place a post's form read your location at (moved a little from where you were), and your theme; while a verification is under way, its check, the ID check's payment, and its gift; and an ID check's payment no stamp has used yet. Never your seed, a key, a stamp or a message.
- **Proofs** run in the background on your device, from files the app serves (about 7 MB, downloaded once).
- **Pay addresses.** Each profile lists its own, for when payments come; the app doesn't show them yet.
- **Same seed, same Forest.** Everything the app writes follows the standard and the reference app in services (`mcp/test/app.ts`) exactly, and the passkeys you add, which the reference app doesn't make, follow the standard's own example for backups, so any app with your seed sees the same profiles, stamps, connections and passkeys.

## Run your own

Run `./standard.sh`, which fetches the standard, the markets and the host's credits client at the commits in `STANDARD`, `MARKETS` and `SERVICES`; then `npm ci` and `npm run dev`. `npm run check && npm test` checks it; `npm run test:browser` runs the whole app in Chromium, with a virtual passkey, against services' own verifier, host, index and connector running on your machine, the way services' loop does (`./standard.sh services` readies them). Node 22.18 or later.

To host a copy, serve the build from any static host with the security headers in `vercel.json`, and set the services it talks to in its one config file. A copy at another address makes its own passkeys, so its own seeds.

## How we run it

On Vercel, at app.forest.foundation. The page talks only to our host, verifier, connector and index, and shows photos from our host's bucket: a strict content security policy lets it reach nothing else. The page loads from the network first, so a new version never leaves you on an old one; a page left open when a new version goes live says so, in one quiet line at the top, with Reload. No analytics and no logs of our own; Vercel keeps its own edge logs.

## Promises

- **It never stores your seed on a device.** Each passkey you add keeps a copy on the host, locked so only that passkey opens it. After an unlock, it holds the seed in the page's memory 5 minutes since the last action, or until the page closes, and stores nothing secret made from it.
- **It moves no money,** and keeps no key of yours past one action.
- **Nothing it writes lets a reader link two of your profiles.**
- **It writes only what the standard and the reference app write,** so any app with your seed finds what this one made.
- **When something can't be done, it says why, in one line.**

## Limits

- The app connects only to our connector, mcp.forest.foundation, for now.
- No push notifications: what's new shows when the app is open.
- While the seed is held, 5 minutes since the last action or until the page closes, it is in the page's memory, where the page's own code reads it without asking; what keeps any other code out is the content security policy.
- Hiding the page doesn't drop the seed: a locked phone or a covered window keeps it until 5 minutes pass with nothing done, so someone who unlocks your device in that time can act as you without the passkey asking.
- What your AI sends shows in your chat, not in the app.
- The host you write to can link your profiles and your passkeys: one device writes them all, one wallet funds them, one inbox lists them. It promises to keep no addresses in its logs; that's its promise, not the app's.
- To show your ratings, the app asks the index about each of your profiles from your device, so the index can link them, as the host already can.
- What's new goes by the time each writer puts on what they write: a review dated in the past shows no dot.
- A web app's code comes from its server each time you open it, so you trust whoever serves it. It's open source, and you can run your own.
- Your seed can't leave this app yet: its passkeys belong to this address, and there's no export. Lose every passkey and the seed is gone.
- Whoever holds any one of your passkeys holds the seed: each is as safe as the manager or key that keeps it.
- Your first passkey can't be removed: it is your seed.
- Removing a passkey takes back nothing it already opened: whoever opened its seed entry has the seed, and so does anyone who opens a copy a host still keeps.
- A passkey's password manager shows by name only on the device that added it, and only when the passkey says which it is; elsewhere it shows by the day it was added.
- On a device that knows a Forest passkey, Start never offers to make another, even when that one was deleted from your manager; clearing this site's data in the browser does.
- One host, ours, for everything you make, and only profiles on it can be messaged from here.
- Anyone can see when one of your profiles keeps a copy of what it sent, though not what it says or whom it's to; someone watching the other person's inbox could match the times.
- A post's place is a point moved a few hundred metres from where you were, kept on this device, and a circle round it that holds your real spot. Another device of yours keeps its own points.
- No map yet: a place's point is where your device is, never a spot you pick, and a post without your location has no place, so a search near a point won't find it.
- Photos show as the host serves them; the app doesn't check them against their names yet.
- Not yet: payments between people, carrying your reputation from one profile to another, moving to another host.
- A face Didit has seen before waits for a person at the foundation.
- A proof takes a few seconds on a phone.

## FAQ

**What if I lose my passkey?**
Start on any device with another of your passkeys: every one opens the same profiles. Add one in Settings, in another password manager, on a second phone or on a security key, and remove a lost one there. Lose every passkey, and nobody can open your profiles again, not even the foundation.

**Is it free?**
The app and the face check are; the ID check is $5. The host charges for what you write, a tiny fraction of a cent per post or message, and your verification comes with a gift that covers thousands of them. Buying more comes later.

**Why do I unlock to post, but not to look?**
Posting needs your seed, to sign as your profile, and your passkey gives it only after you unlock. The app then holds it 5 minutes since the last action, or until the page closes, so posting again soon asks nothing. Looking needs nothing: everything your profiles show is public, and your device remembers which ones are yours. Messages are the one thing locked to you, so opening them takes an unlock, unless the seed is held.

**Where does my rating come from?**
From an index, ours here: it reads the reviews your profiles received, weighs each by how much it trusts the reviewer, and gives each profile its rating. Each index rates its own way, so another may show another number.

**Who sees my face?**
Didit, the provider our verifier uses, and for the ID check, your document too. The verifier reads the result, turns your selfie into numbers that go into your stamp, keeps none of it, and deletes the check at Didit an hour later; Didit keeps a template of your face so the same face can't be stamped twice. The app never sees any of it, and your profiles show only how you're verified. The details: [services/verifier](https://github.com/foundationforest/services/blob/main/verifier/README.md).

**What can my AI do?**
Act for the profiles you ticked: read, post and message as them, with the tools your AI app lets it use. It never gets your seed, a main key or your addresses. In Settings, untick a profile or Disconnect it any time: it stops at once, and what it wrote stays.
