# app

The Forest app is your control panel for Forest: where you create your profiles, get verified, post, keep up with your feedback and messages, and connect your AI. Your profiles and everything they post live on hosts, open for anyone to read; the app reads them and writes for you. Your device keeps addresses and settings, never your seed, its 24 words, a key, a stamp or a message: the full list is under Behind the scenes.

Your seed is what all your profiles come from. It's kept by your passkey, in the password manager you choose. To act, you unlock: the app asks your passkey for the seed, and you allow it with your device's unlock (face, fingerprint or PIN). A button that will ask shows the unlock icon, a fingerprint, before its word, but on Start and its questions, where every button asks, and on Get verified's cards' buttons and a list of profiles to tick, where the card or the row is the choice itself.

The app is open source, so anyone can run one. The Forest Foundation runs this one, at https://app.forest.foundation. It's a web app for now; phone apps may come later, from the same code.

## How it works

Three places: **Home**, **Messages** and **You**. On a phone they are three tabs at the bottom; on a laptop, a sidebar, with your profiles too. Its first line is the mark alone, a link to Home, with Search Forest beside it filling the rest; then Home, Messages and You; then Profiles, with a small outlined + New at its right for a new one, then every profile, in Home's order, the sidebar scrolling on its own when they run long. On a laptop every screen is one column, centred, but Home's blocks, which fill the width, a profile's tiles, Messages, in two panes, and an offer, in three. Changing one thing (a passkey's name, an AI app's profiles, naming someone, your email address) opens in place, under what opened it, and a second tap closes it; a short choice or a confirmation (a market's roles, the made sheet, Add manually, Which profiles?, Delete this profile?, Report) opens as a sheet from the bottom, the screen dimmed behind it, centred with a round × on a laptop; a check opens in the box; anything with steps or to read (Your 24 recovery words, Your AI, Email, an ID's page, Privacy, Terms, Your data) stays a page of its own; a profile's ··· menu opens under its button, and a tap outside closes it. A listing's fields open in place, one at a time; removing something (a passkey, an AI app, a listing or a review, your email, Forget this device) asks again in its own place, with Cancel; Start's questions take its button's place. Buttons are pills; a button with an icon alone is round. There are two greens, in each look, and nothing else is green:

   - **Strong green** does something: a screen's one main action (Create, Post, Save, Continue, Home's New), at most one a screen but Connect your AI's two, Add to ChatGPT and Add to Claude, side by side, and Send. If it is strong green, tapping it acts. It is also the ink on the soft green.
   - **Soft green** is picked, yours or done: where you are (the tab, the sidebar's place, the conversation open), a picked choice, chip or tick, your own messages (their words in black), Verified, Yours.
   - **Black** is for words, counts, the new dot and the check badges (the black squares). Every other action is outlined in black, on what is behind it: Review (its star in the sun), the sidebar's + New, a second choice; a text link is underlined in black. **Yellow** means it needs you: the ! where something needs updating, Needs updating, Sync profiles, a check's answer and the stars. **Dashed** means not yet, tap to add: a check badge you don't have. **White** is cards and fields only, and **red** removes and reports. Start sits on the strong green, and in the dark look on the soft one; dark mode flips the two greens.

What's new reads one way everywhere. A count on what you tap is how many things inside it have something new, never how many messages, up to 9+: on the Messages tab, the conversations with something new, across your profiles; on a profile (its card on Home, its row in the sidebar), its conversations with something new and its new reviews, vouches and reports; on a profile's messages button, its conversations; on an offer, the conversations about it; beside a rating, "1 new", up to "9+ new". A message hides who sent it until your seed opens it, so new messages not opened yet show as the black dot in the count's place, until they open: with your seed held, they open by themselves. A yellow ! takes the count's place, in the same corner, when something there needs updating: a listing, or a review you wrote, that its market outgrew. In a list, each new item has a dot and bold words. A card or row that opens what it shows has no mark; a pencil means a tap edits what you wrote, and then the whole card is the button; a caret shows only on a settings row, which opens a page; round arrows do something now. Someone's short key is the first 6 characters of their address and "…". A name you give someone is at most 12 characters, never cut (one saved longer before shows its first 12 and "…" until edited); titles end in "…" where they run long. Your profiles keep the order they were made in; every other list puts what is new or needs you first, newest first, then the rest, newest first (newest is a conversation's last message, sent or received, an offer's, or when it was posted or last edited, a review's when it was written), and keeps its order while it's on screen.

1. **Start.** One button, Start, white, on the strong green with the mark and Forest, on a phone and a laptop alike. It asks for a passkey: the one this device used, else any Forest passkey. The browser's own window offers its other ways, a phone's QR code among them; the app never redraws them. A passkey that answers takes you in: to Home, or to where setup was left. What Start says next takes its button's place:
   - **Start found nothing**, when nothing comes back (no passkey, or the window closed, which a browser can't tell apart), with a back arrow, top left: I'm new, white, makes your passkey (your password manager keeps it, and with it your seed; one that still keeps this device's Forest passkey says so, and points to the other button), then Get verified; I already use Forest, outlined, asks for any passkey again. Every passkey you've added opens the same profiles.
   - **No passkey found**, when I already use Forest finds nothing: Try again, or Type my 24 words, the one door to the words: type the 24 words, in order, and the app makes a new passkey that opens your seed from then on, as one added in You does, and you're in. Quietly under them, Back goes back to Start's button. Continue waits for all 24; words that can't be used say why in one line before any passkey is made; words that open a different Forest from the one this device lists are refused, and Forget this device comes first, so one device never mixes two seeds.
   - **This passkey has never been verified**, for a passkey met at Start (not one made in this visit) whose seed holds no stamp and no profiles, with no check under way here: "Verified with another passkey? Delete this one." Continue to verification, white, goes on to Get verified; Delete passkey, red, tells the browser it's gone (`signalUnknownCredential`), forgets this device and that passkey, and goes back to Start.
   - **Save a passkey on this device?**, once, after a passkey on another device (a phone through the QR code) let a verified person in, so each unlock here doesn't ask that phone again: Save makes one here and adds it; Not now goes on.
   - **Passkeys don't work here**, in place of all of it, in a browser where they can't (no WebAuthn, no PRF, or an app's own browser: Instagram's, Facebook's, TikTok's, LinkedIn's, Snapchat's): "Open Forest in a different browser.", and Copy link, white, which copies the page's address and says Copied a moment.

2. **Get verified.** The first time, a verifier checks that you're a real person it hasn't seen before, and signs a stamp to your seed, so people know you're real. A page you come back to: a back arrow, Get verified, "So people know you're real.", and two cards, Face first, then Face + ID. While a check is to do, its card has its badge (the black square), its name and one line, its price at the right in black, what it gives with a tick each, then its button, strong green, the card's whole width:
   - **Face, Free.** "Quick start", with free credits. Take a selfie opens its box.
   - **Face + ID, one time.** "Have an official ID in hand", at the right the price the verifier says (`GET /`, `id.price`: $5) with "one time" small under it: so many times the credits (the ratio of the ID's gift to the face's, ten times), higher trust (indexes can count it as stronger), and it supports Forest, funding the foundation. Verify with your ID opens its box.

   Credit words show only where the verifier gives credits at this host. While the verifier's payments are test payments, which charge nobody (`testPayments`), the ID card says so in one quiet line: "Test mode: pay with the card 4242 4242 4242 4242." The ID check shows only while the verifier sells it (`forSale`): until then there is no ID card and no ID outline beside Profiles or You, and an ID check you already have still shows as Verified. A check done shrinks to its badge and its name, with a soft green "✓ Verified" at its right. The face check is free once (`POST /stamp`'s `faceTriesLeft`): with no free check left and no face stamp, the Face card is greyed, its name alone. From Start, once a check is done, Continue at the foot: an underlined link while the ID is still to do, strong green once both are done; back is gone, since being verified can't be undone. From You or Home, Get verified has Back and no Continue. Someone whose earlier stamps all count for nothing, since the verifier changed its keys, has their profiles listed, then comes here, one line at the top saying "Your earlier check no longer counts: the verifier changed its keys. Check again.", until a check is done; then Continue goes Home. Until then New profile makes nothing, and says "Get verified first: your earlier check no longer counts."

   **A check happens in one box:** a white card with rounded corners filling the screen (on a laptop, the side beside the Start panel, or beside the sidebar), under a round × at its top left. All of the check happens inside it, one step after another, and the app never leaves the page, so the seed held at its start is still held at its end and nothing asks the passkey again:
   1. **Before**: "Before your face check" (or "Before your ID check"); the verifier's own words as it serves them in `GET /` (`face.consent`, or `consent` for the ID), scrolling, with a fade at their foot; above a line, one tick holding its line to agree to (`agree`), and for the ID its right to cancel (`id.cancelRight`) right after it, in the same tick, unticked at first; Start, or Continue to pay, strong green, working once ticked, the unlock icon on it while no seed is held; under it, small, Terms · Privacy, each the site's page in a new tab.
   2. **Paying**, the ID check only: the verifier's payment page framed in the box (`/id/pay#<clientSecret>`, its client secret after the #, which no request carries). The person pays there, on Stripe's embedded page, which holds the price on their card and takes it only for a stamp. Its one message, "payment_done", taken from the verifier's origin and that frame alone, moves on: the session opens with the payment, and the verifier asks Stripe whether the money is held. A paid payment still waiting, under a day old, skips this step.
   3. **Didit**: the session's own page framed in the box, with what a camera check needs. Its retakes happen inside it. Its messages are taken from Didit's origin and that frame alone: the moment didit:completed comes, the frame goes, so Didit's own result screen never shows (the verifier may still refuse what Didit approved); didit:cancelled or didit:close_request close the box.
   4. **Checking**: the loader and Checking in the middle of the box while the stamp is asked for, again while it isn't finished.
   5. Then back to Get verified, the card Verified; or an answer, in the box.

   The × closes the box at any step: a check left unfinished uses no try, and a payment made waits. Tapped again, a check whose session is kept opens at Checking once, then its page, as it was. Only the verifier's answer counts. Each answer is a yellow round !, a bold title, one line and its buttons:
   - the face, a face seen before: "Someone with a similar face is already verified" / "If it's you, sign in with your other passkey. If not, verify with your ID.", with Use my other passkey (from Start only: it asks for the passkey you use, adds this one to it, and you're in) and Verify with your ID;
   - the face, too young: "We couldn't confirm you're 18 or older" / "Try with your ID instead."; any other refusal, or the free check used: "Too many failed tries" / "Verify with your ID instead.", each with Verify with your ID, which opens the ID's Before in the same box;
   - the ID, one it couldn't accept, while the payment has a session left: "We couldn't accept this ID" / "Try again, or with a different ID.", with Keep trying, a new session in the box, and Skip, you won't be charged, which cancels the payment; on its last session: "Too many failed tries" / "You weren't charged.";
   - the ID, a person seen before: "You're already verified with another passkey" / "Sign in with it. You weren't charged.", with Delete this passkey (as on Start, only while this passkey holds no stamp; else Close) and Lost your passkey? Contact us; too young: "Forest is for people 18 and older" / "You weren't charged.";
   - either, an error of the app's or a service's: "Something went wrong" / "It wasn't you. Try again.", with Try again, the step again. A session that ran out goes back to Get verified, and so does a payment that is gone, the ID ready to pay.

   Each check is opened for your stamp number, the number your seed gives at the verifier, and gives a stamp to that number alone. Both run with Didit, the identity-check company our verifier uses. The ID check gives a face stamp too, from the same selfie, when your stamp number holds none. With each stamp comes the list of what the check saw, its facts, and a gift of credits, which pay the host for what you write. All go into your Profiles folder, a private folder only your seed opens, so any device you start on finds them.

   **Add a backup**, from Start, once a device, after a check is done (skipped for a seed that has a backup passkey already): a back arrow, Add a backup, "If you lose your passkey, it's the only way back.", and two cards. Another passkey, "Not in" wherever the first one is (its maker's manager: "Apple Passwords", "Google Password Manager", or the maker's own name; "Not where your first one is" when the first passkey named no maker): Add a backup passkey, strong green, as You's Passkeys adds one; one from the same maker as the first is dropped before anything is written, and the page comes back with one yellow note, "That one is in Apple Passwords too. Save it somewhere else.". 24 recovery words, "Written down on paper": Show my words, outlined, asks the passkey and opens Your 24 recovery words: "Write them on paper, in order. Anyone with them can open your Forest.", the words in three numbered columns, and I wrote them down, strong green. Under the cards, Later. A backup added, the words written down, or Later: on to your first profiles, and setup never shows this page again on this device.

   **Your first profiles**: every market as a tile, live and beta alike, two to a row (on a laptop, as many columns of about 170px as fit), about 104px tall, its corners rounded: the market's color fills it, under a soft dark fade behind the words (in the dark look, a layer of black over all of it too); its icon big, white and faint, at the top right, cut by the tile's edge; its whole name in white at the bottom left, large, so it reads on every color; at its top left a round mark, a + while none of its roles is yours, a ✓ once one is, with the names of the roles you have small under the market's name. Over them, a search, over the markets' names and their roles' words. Tap one: a sheet of its roles, the market's icon white on its color, its name, under it Beta for a beta market, then the market's own summary (the one line its file gives for where someone picks a market). Then each role a row, its avatar and its name, and at its right Create, strong green, which makes it there, the sheet staying, or, for a role you have, a round soft green ✓ that opens it. Continue, strong green, at the foot, always, since profiles are optional, goes on to Connect your AI (8), whose back goes on to Home.

   On a laptop, Start and its screens stay full screen on the strong green, as on a phone. Every setup screen after Start (Get verified and its box, Add a backup, Your first profiles, Connect your AI) splits the window: the strong green panel on the left with the mark and Forest, the screen on the right. Sheets open centred, with a round × at their top right.

3. **Home.** Your profiles. At the top, on a phone, the mark and Search Forest, which opens the index (on a laptop it is in the sidebar). Then Profiles, with your badges beside it, once, since every profile of yours carries the same checks: a badge for each, the black square, a face being scanned for the face check, an ID card for the ID check, each saying so when tapped (Verified with Face, Verified with Face + ID); a check you haven't done shows as its badge's outline, dashed (the ID's only while the verifier sells it), and a tap opens Get verified. At its right, while a market's role has no profile of yours, New, a small strong green pill with its +, Home's one action. On a phone, one card per profile, in the order they were made: its avatar (its market's icon on its market's color, solid when it offers, light when it requests) with its mark on the avatar's corner (its conversations with something new and its new reviews, vouches and reports; the dot while new messages aren't opened yet; the ! while something of it needs updating), its market in bold and its role small under it, and at its right its rating over how many reviews it has, or No reviews; every card one height, rated or not, and no offers under them. On a laptop, the profiles in columns of about 300px, each a block: its header on a light tint of its market's color, its avatar with no mark (the sidebar holds the profile's), its market and role, its rating; under it its offers or requests as lines, each its photo, title and price and its own mark, every one with something new first, then others up to two in all, then how many more ("2 more"), which opens the profile; or No offers yet. When your Profiles folder holds profiles made on another device, one yellow line under the title, Sync profiles, with round arrows at its right: a tap asks your passkey once, the arrows spin, then the line goes and their cards show; any unlock for anything else does it by itself. To make a new profile, tap New: New profile, with a back arrow, the same tiles and sheets as your first profiles; Create beside a role, and unlock unless your seed is held: the app makes it right there, proving it comes from a verified seed without saying which, and the sheet stays, the row its ✓, which opens it. When an AI app acts for some of your profiles, Create turns the sheet into what was made: a round soft green ✓ and "Tutor profile created", the market under it; "Your AI can use it" ("Your AIs can use it" for more than one); each AI app a row, its name and a round tick, on, a tap turning one off; Done, strong green, gives the new profile to each app ticked, as Your AI's Save does, the seed held, and closes. While a profile is made, and while your messages are read, the loader shows once the wait passes half a second, alone in the middle; nothing else waits behind it. A profile whose market is gone, one the directory no longer lists (a test profile from the old markets, or one in goods or software, now Goods selling and Software development, new markets with new labels), is forgotten by the device and shown nowhere; nothing is deleted at the host.

4. **Listings.** Post an offer or a request from a profile, as its role posts. The form is the market's own file, read as it is, in the market's own words: first the listing's own words, About, an open box, since the market asks them first and their first line is the listing's title; then each field a row, its label, then your answer in words, or the market's question in grey while it has none; tap a row and it opens under itself, with the market's hint, its input and Done. A question that depends on earlier answers shows once they hold, and goes when a changed answer means it no longer applies, so Goods selling asks what is sold, and for clothes who wears them and their sizes, and for a child's, their age. On a laptop, How it reads sits beside the form: the listing as others will see it, filling in as you go. Each kind of answer has one input, the same in every market. **Text**, a line, or a box where the market says it runs long. **A choice**, side by side, or chips, a tick on each picked where it takes several, picking one unpicking the other where it takes one; a long list, such as languages, a search, each value by its name, never a code (Spanish, never es). **A number**, with − and + beside it and its unit after it, one or many as the number says (1 hour, 3 hours); where the market asks it as a range, From and To, either left empty for no limit, said as the markets say it: "9 to 11 years", "18 years or more", "Up to 11 years". **Spots**, in a market that asks them, by its word for them (Guests, Learners, Group size, Volunteers): how many places an offer has, or how many people a request is for, one whole number, 0 when full. **A price** is a list of one or more: each an amount in its currency, then what it's for, "for 1 hour", the length and its unit from what the market prices by (hours or minutes, days, months, people), or the whole job; a few words of note if you want ("at least 2 hours"). Minutes are held as hours, exactly, so they go in steps of 3: 45 minutes is 0.75 of an hour. It reads as people say it, "€15 an hour", "€60 for 3 hours", "€25 for 45 minutes"; a request's is the most you'd pay, "Up to €60 for 3 hours". Add another price adds one, and each has a remove button while there are two or more; where a listing shows small, as on its card, it shows the first price and how many more (+1 more). **A place** is a circle: a map with the circle fixed in its middle, which you move under it; Search a place, by its name in any language (places, from GeoNames and Correos de México); the map's own button for your location; and a slider for its size, all the way left an exact spot, then 1 km up to 100 km, starting where the market starts it for your role, an exact spot for some, a wide circle for others. Under it, the place in words, its size and the name places gives the circle's middle, "Within 10 km of Chamberí, Madrid": shown, never stored, since a listing's place is three numbers and no name; with no answer from places, its size alone. Where your browser hasn't been told no, the place asks for your location as it opens, so a circle drops round you with nothing to type; an exact spot is never set from your location alone, since that would be your door, so the map goes there and waits for you to move it to the spot. A circle is posted with its centre moved by up to a tenth of its radius, the same way every time for that profile and size, worked out from your seed, so your listings never average out to your door and nothing of where you are is kept on the device; one an edit keeps, or your AI wrote, is posted as it is. An exact spot, for a public place, is posted exactly where the map's middle is, and says anyone can see it. A field that holds several places takes several, each in a list by its name, with a remove button, and Add another area. Your location only centres the map: the app asks for it as a place opens, or when you tap the map's button, and the browser asks you first. **Days and times** are a list of times, Add a time where the market takes several: each with an Every week switch, on with its weekday buttons and Until, no end unless you set a day; off, a day, or From and To days, or By, as the market asks; then its hours, from one exact time to another, never "mornings"; your device's time zone named under them, and held with the hours; and where the listing holds spots, Advanced, a quiet link at the time's foot, opens that time's own spots ("Guests at this time"), which win for that time: "Only for this time. 0 when it's full."; open by itself when the time holds some. A time reads with them: "Saturdays, 10:00 to 12:00, 3 spots", or full. Days that end before they start are said in one line before anything is posted. **Photos**, as tiles; **a link**, a web address. In a beta market, the form says once, at its top, "Beta: it works, at your own risk, and may change." A listing can carry a contact (a phone, a handle, an email): it is public, and the same contact on two listings links them. You can edit or delete a listing; Delete asks again, in its own place. When a market changes (only a beta market can) and a listing of yours no longer fits it, the app finds it by itself, from your listings and the market files it already reads: the yellow ! on its profile's card on Home and in the sidebar, and on its own card, which says Needs updating in yellow in its price's place. Its edit, from its page, opens with every answer that still fits filled in, one yellow note at its top, "This market changed. Answers to redo are in yellow.", and each row whose answer didn't in yellow, "Changed: answer again." until you answer it; posted, it replaces the old one in place, and the marks go. A listing that no longer fits never breaks a screen, and one shown from the index shows without the parts that don't fit.

5. **A profile.** Tap its card and its page opens, its header on a light tint of its market's color. At the top, Back, and at the right, round, its messages, with its conversations' mark, and its ···, a small menu under it: Share profile, which hands out its page on the index, for anyone who wants to message or review it (the phone's share sheet, else the link copied, the ··· saying Copied a moment), and Delete profile, red. Delete profile opens a sheet, "Delete this profile?": "Your profile and its listings disappear from Forest.", "Reviews people left you don't go away.", "If you make this profile again, it comes back with the same reviews."; Delete profile, red, and Cancel. Then every live entry in its folder is deleted at the host, its profile entry last, it is taken off every AI app and the morning email, its label goes out of your Profiles folder, the device forgets it, and you land on Home. Made again, the same market and role make the same profile, at the same address. Then its avatar, its market big, its role small with its badges right after it, and the rating an index gives it (ours here) with how many reviews, or with no score yet what came (1 vouch), or No reviews yet, on a pill that says "1 new" while something came that you haven't seen, and carries the ! while a review it wrote needs updating; the pill opens its Reviews. Then each of its offers or requests (as its role posts), a card of its own with gaps between, what is new or needs you first: its first photo, else its market's icon, its mark on the corner, its title on one line, and its first price at the right with how many more (+1 more), or Needs updating in yellow in its place; no description, no pencil, no tabs. At the foot, New offer (or New request), strong green; with none, No offers yet (or No requests yet), quiet, in the middle. On a laptop, its offers are a grid of photo tiles, each with its mark in the corner, its title and price, and New offer sits beside the Offers heading.

   **An offer's page**, from its card: Back and Share (round) at the top; the offer as one card, its photo, title, price or Needs updating, and a pencil at the right, the whole card opening its edit; under it, Messages and the conversations about it, as Messages shows them but without its title, what is new first; No messages yet, quiet, in the middle; or, before your messages are opened, the lock and Open. On a phone it shows no details and no description. On a laptop, three panes: the offer, big, on the left (its photos one at a time, "1 of 4", its title, its price, its answers in words, its place, its description, and Edit), its conversations beside it, and the one you tap opens beside them, the offer staying.

   **Reviews**, from the rating: Back and Reviews; one line with the profile, its avatar small, its market and role, and at its right "You wrote 2 ›", an underlined link to the reviews it wrote, with the ! while one needs updating; the score large and how many; then what the index counted about it, reviews, vouches and reports together, the new ones first with their dot, newest first, each with who wrote it, by your name for them or their key, opening in full, with From, a row with who wrote it that opens their profile, then Message, Review back and Report; "No reviews yet" when nothing came. Once shown there, they are seen, and the profile counts them new no more. **You wrote**: Back and You wrote, then each review, vouch or report the profile wrote, a card: About, the avatar of who it's about and your name for them or their key, a pencil at the right, then its score or chip and its words, and Needs updating in yellow when its market outgrew it; the whole card opens it, to edit or delete, with what still fits filled in. You start a review from a conversation, someone's profile, or a review you received. Then one of three: a score from 1 to 10, in tenths, with the market's own questions, where it asks any; a vouch, that they're real and honest, without a score; or a report, that they're fake or a scam. None names which of their listings it's about. A review another app or your AI wrote may name it (its `listing`, the listing's full address, `<profile address>/listing/<id>`): when the listing is its subject's or its writer's, wherever a review received shows (the reviews a profile received, one opened, someone else's page), an About box under its words shows the listing, its first photo (else its market's icon on its color), its title and price, a tap opening it on the index; one the index no longer lists says "No longer listed". Each is about someone on the other side of the same market (in a market with one role, like language exchange, the same role), never one of your own profiles, and the app checks that before you unlock, in one line. Written again about the same person, a review, vouch or report goes in place of the one before: an index counts one of each.

6. **Messages.** The middle place: every profile's conversations, what is new first, newest first, then the rest, with chips, small, on one row that scrolls sideways: All, then each of your profiles by its market, in the order they were made, a dot in its market's color (Home cleaning), its role added only where two of yours share a market (Goods selling seller, Goods selling buyer); the one picked on the soft green. A profile's messages button opens them on its chip, with Back to the profile. A message hides who sent it until your seed opens it, so opening them takes one unlock, and a tap into Messages (the tab, the sidebar's Messages, a profile's messages button) asks your passkey itself, as it goes there. ## Behind the scenes

- **Your seed** is your first passkey's PRF secret: a value the passkey gives only after you unlock, the same 32 bytes on every device it syncs to. After an unlock, the app holds it in the page's memory 15 minutes since the last action, or until the page closes, or until Forget. What the page does by itself (opening messages after an unlock and as new ones come, reading the Profiles folder after an unlock) uses the seed held but doesn't count as an action. A page only hidden (another window over it, another tab, the phone locked) keeps it, and an action already running finishes first. It is never stored on a device, nor anything secret made from it. Each passkey you add keeps a copy on the host, locked so only that passkey opens it.
- **Your 24 words** are your seed itself, as the standard spells it (`exportWords`): BIP39's words for its 32 bytes, without BIP39's passphrase step. Their page, from Passkeys and from Add a backup, shows them after a fresh unlock, even when the seed is held, and only while it is open; they live in the page's memory while it is, never its history, the seed they came from is dropped at once, and a reload there asks again. Type my 24 words, under Start's No passkey found, reads them back (`importWords`) and checks, before any passkey is made: all 24, each in its place; verified, a stamp in their Profiles folder, whose wallet pays for the new passkey's folder; and the Forest this device lists, if it lists one. Words that spell an added passkey's own seed are followed one step, to the seed it opens. Then the app makes a passkey and adds it as below, so from then on it opens your seed.
- **Your stamp** is the verifier's signature over your stamp number and its facts: one number made from the list of what the check saw (for the face check, the face model's name and the numbers it gave for your face; for the ID, those and every text field Didit read on your document, your name, birth date and document number among them), by the standard's recipe. The verifier hands you the list once, beside the stamp, and keeps only the number. The ID check gives a face stamp too, from the same selfie, when your stamp number holds none, and the app keeps both. The app keeps both, as the reference app keeps a stamp, in a locked `stamp/<id>` entry of your Profiles folder, so only your seed opens it and you can later show any of those facts to someone you choose (the standard's facts proof; the app makes none yet). A stamp asked back by its number comes without its list, and never takes the place of one kept with it. Your stamp number is the number your seed gives at the verifier's name, the only thing made from your seed it ever sees.
- **Names you give others** live in one locked entry of your Profiles folder, the app's own, at `names`: `{ "names": [{ "address": <their profile's address>, "name": <your name for it> }] }`, locked to the folder's own key alone, so only your seed opens it and any device you unlock on gets them. Saving reads the entry as the host holds it, changes one name and writes it again, paid from your wallet; taking back the last deletes it. The standard leaves paths like it to the app that writes them, and other apps ignore it. It is never in a profile's folder, never sent to an index, never handed to your AI, whose access keys open profiles, not this folder. The device keeps a copy, so names show with nothing unlocked, and every unlock that opens your Profiles folder puts the entry's in its place.
- **The morning email** is the host's: once a day, in your morning, it emails you a count of what's new on the profiles you ticked, with a button that opens the app, and never what a message says, since it cannot open one. Turning it on is two calls: each ticked profile's main key signs your email address for this host (services' `host/client/email.ts`, used as it is), the host emails a 6-digit code, good for 15 minutes, five wrong ones deleting it, and answers an id; the code and the id turn it on. The id, your email address and the addresses of the profiles you ticked live in one locked entry of your Profiles folder, the app's own, at `email`: `{ "id", "email", "profiles": [address] }`, locked to the folder's own key alone, written and deleted the way `names` is, so only your seed opens it and every device you unlock on shows the same state. The id is never in a URL; the host puts it in the stop link of its own emails. It is never in a profile's folder, never sent to an index, never handed to your AI. The device keeps a copy, so Every morning or Off shows with nothing unlocked, and every unlock that opens your Profiles folder puts the entry's in its place. A new email address is turned on as the first was; once it is on, the old one is stopped. In the host's own words: **with email on, this host learns which profiles are one person's, and that person's email address. It publishes neither: readers and indexes still see each profile on its own.** It keeps, until it is turned off: for each email address turned on, its id, the email address, its time zone, the profiles' addresses, when it was confirmed and when it last sent; until it is confirmed, its code, when that was sent and the wrong tries, all deleted if that is not within 15 minutes; and in memory only, for a day, when codes went to each email address, so nobody floods one. Turning it off deletes all of it, at once, from the app or the stop link. It keeps no count: each morning it works them out from the inbox entries and entries it holds anyway. Resend, which sends it, sees each email: the email address, the counts and the profiles' names.
- **Passkeys you add.** Each one after the first is what the standard calls a backup: it gives a seed of its own, whose Profiles folder holds yours in its [seed entry](https://github.com/foundationforest/standard/blob/main/folders/README.md#the-seed-entry), locked to that folder alone, as the standard says. Adding one writes a `backup/<id>` entry in your Profiles folder, holding its seed, locked the same way; then its own folder, its first write paid with a credit from your wallet. If that fails, the `backup/<id>` entry is deleted again, and one red line says the passkey wasn't added. Every way of adding one does the same: from Passkeys, to save one on this device, or from your 24 words. Every unlock follows a backup to your seed, one step and never more, so a passkey added or removed on another device counts at once; one that no longer opens the profiles this device lists (one removed since) stops, in one red line. Removing one deletes its seed entry, then its `backup/<id>` entry. The entry holds the seed and nothing else: where a passkey lives, from an id each new passkey sends naming its maker, and the name you give it stay on the device that added or renamed it.
- **The passkey belongs to app.forest.foundation,** so no other site can ask it for your seed, not even another page under forest.foundation.
- **Credits.** The host charges by the byte, a tiny fraction of a cent per listing or message, a little more for a photo. Each new profile gets a credit from your wallet, your Profiles folder's balance, and when one runs low the app tops it up. The host sees the wallet pay for each top-up but can't tell which profile it went to: it signed the credits blind.
- **A profile deleted** (its ···, Delete profile): first its AI apps (one that acts for it alone is disconnected, so no connection is left that its notes can't find again; any other stops acting for it), then the morning email (it counts the rest, or, with none left, it is turned off), then every live entry in its folder deleted at the host, signed by its main key, its profile entry last: its listings, the reviews, vouches and reports it wrote, its AI apps' notes, the locked copies of what it sent. Deletes over live entries are free at the host. Then each `label/<id>` naming it goes out of your Profiles folder, and the device forgets it. The reviews, vouches and reports others wrote about it live in their folders and stay; the seed gives the same address for the same market and role, so making it again makes the same profile, and they come back with it.
- **Your AI's connection** is a folder like any other, holding its access keys instead of listings, named by a random key, so its name links to nothing. It holds the AI app's name and, for each profile you ticked, that profile's access key, all locked to the connector and the folder alone; the AI app's login carries a pointer to it, locked to the connector. Each profile ticked lists its key in its profile entry, with a note only your seed opens naming the folder, so any device with your seed finds your AI apps again: at Start, and on a device in use at its next unlock, once a profile it reads lists a key it doesn't know. That reads each of your profiles' folders and each AI app's folder their notes name, in the background; reading is free, and nothing is written. The folder's first write is paid from your wallet. Unticking a profile ends its key; Disconnect deletes the connection, then ends every key it held.
- **Avatars.** Your own profiles show their market's icon, wherever they show (Home, the sidebar, a profile's header, the ticks for an AI app), so you tell them apart by their markets; in Messages, the same icon is the badge on the picture of whoever the conversation is with. Anyone else's profile is an animal, one of forty, picked on your device from the profile's address, so the same profile is the same animal on every device and for everyone using this app; nothing is stored or published. It's this app's own drawing: another app, or the index, may draw a profile its own way (the index shows its market's icon). A market's icon is the one its market file names, a [Phosphor](https://phosphoricons.com) icon, on tiles and a market's page too, and the app carries only the icons its markets name; a market that names none, or one the app doesn't draw, shows its name's first letter. Its color is its number, from its market file, cycled through the index's ten colors in the index's order (services' `index/src/avatar.ts`), so a market is the same color in the app and on the index, and markets numbered next to each other differ. The animals are Microsoft's [Fluent Emoji](https://github.com/microsoft/fluentui-emoji), High Contrast, MIT licensed.
- **Type.** Figtree sets every word, titles at its heaviest. The name Forest, where the app writes it (Start, the setup panel), is drawn once from Fraunces, as the landing page sets it, so no other font is downloaded. Both under the SIL Open Font License.
- **Photos** are redrawn on your device before they're sent: a JPEG, no side longer than 1600 pixels, nothing but the pixels. So a photo's location, and the rest of what a camera writes into it, never leaves your device. A listing names each photo by its fingerprint (SHA-256); the host keeps it while a listing names it, and serves it to anyone.
- **Ratings** come from an index, ours here, which reads every host, counts verified profiles, and rates each one from the reviews it counts, weighing each by how much it trusts the reviewer. Each index rates its own way, so another may show another number. The app asks it, from your device, what it says of each of your profiles. A review's score is the reviewer's; the rating is the index's.
- **What the device keeps:** the id of the passkey in use and of each one it has made or used, your profiles' labels and addresses, your Profiles folder's address and the label entries it has read there, with when each says its profile was made, how you're verified (worked out again from your Profiles folder's stamps at Start, after each check and after each unlock), of each ID stamp the kind of document and its own name for it, its country, when it runs out (a copy of four of its facts, so Your checks lists them with nothing unlocked; never your name, birth date or document number) and the path of its entry in your Profiles folder, your AI apps (each one's name, the day it connected, its folder's address and its profiles), the addresses of the access keys it knows of, where you left off in each inbox, the time of the newest review, vouch or report about each profile you've seen, where each passkey it made or added lives and the name you gave any, the names you gave others, the morning email if it's on (its id, your email address and the profiles it counts), the centre of each area you've put on the map (moved a little from where you put it), whether setup has offered Add a backup here, and your theme; while a verification is under way, its check and its session, the ID check's payment and how many tries it opened, and the buy for its gift; and an ID check's payment no stamp has used yet. Never your seed, its 24 words, a key, a stamp or a message.
- **Stamps that count** are the ones from the verifier's current keys, as it lists them: one from a key it no longer signs with (either key before services#144, when it began signing the standard's facts as a list; its ID key before services#123) counts for nothing. The app shows no check for it, and no profile carries a new verification of it. Someone whose stamps all count for nothing checks again, as Get verified says; then every profile shows the new stamp beside the old.
- **Proofs** run in the background on your device, from files the app serves (about 7 MB, downloaded once).
- **Pay addresses.** Each profile lists its own, for when payments come; the app doesn't show them yet.
- **A message's listing.** A message's body is the two apps' to agree, as the standard says: `{ "text": <its words> }`. The first message written from a listing adds `listing` beside `text`: the listing's full address, `<profile address>/listing/<id>`, the shape a review's `listing` has; its locked copy in the sender's folder holds it too, so every device shows the listing. Later messages carry only `text`. The app shows a conversation's listing only when it is under one of its two profiles' addresses, and a review's only when it is under its subject's or its writer's; any other is left out. It is a convention between this app and services' MCP server, which hands it to your AI with the message; services' reference app (`mcp/test/app.ts`) sends no messages, and a `listing` of any other shape is left out. The listing's photo, title, price and whether it is still listed come from the index's page for it (`/profiles/<profile address>/listing/<id>.json`), read once a page.
- **Same seed, same Forest.** Everything the app writes follows the standard and the reference app in services (`mcp/test/app.ts`) exactly, and the passkeys you add, which the reference app doesn't make, follow the standard's own example for backups, so any app with your seed sees the same profiles, stamps, connections and passkeys.

## Run your own

Run `./standard.sh`, which fetches the standard, the markets and services (for the host's credits client) at the commits in `STANDARD`, `MARKETS` and `SERVICES`; then `npm ci` and `npm run dev`. To test it, run `./standard.sh services` instead, which also readies services' own verifier, host, index and connector to run on your machine, as services' end-to-end test runs them. Then `npm run check` checks the types, `npm test` the flows against those services, and `npm run test:browser` the whole app in Chromium, with a virtual passkey, against them too. Node 22.18 or later.

When services moves its `MARKETS`, the app follows by itself within a day: `MARKETS` to services' and `SERVICES` to services' main, once every check here passes with them ([`markets.yml`](.github/workflows/markets.yml)); `STANDARD` moves by hand, here and in services. To move them now, Run workflow on [its page](https://github.com/foundationforest/app/actions/workflows/markets.yml).

To host a copy, serve the build from any static host with the security headers in `vercel.json`, and set the services it talks to in its one config file. A copy at another address makes its own passkeys, so its own seeds.

## How we run it

On Vercel, at app.forest.foundation. The page frames only the verifier's payment page, Didit's check page and the site's privacy and terms pages, and talks only to our host, verifier, connector and index, and, where you set a place, places, for its search and its name, and OpenFreeMap, for the map; it shows photos from our host's bucket: a strict content security policy lets it reach nothing else. The page loads from the network first, so a new version never leaves you on an old one; a page left open when a new version goes live says so, in one quiet line at the top, with Reload. ## Promises

- **It never stores your seed on a device,** nor its 24 words, nor anything secret made from it. The words show only after an unlock, while their screen is open. After an unlock, it holds the seed in the page's memory 15 minutes since the last action, or until the page closes. Each passkey you add keeps a copy on the host, locked so only that passkey opens it.
- **It moves no money,** and keeps no key of yours past one action.
- **Nothing it writes lets a reader link two of your profiles.**
- **It writes only what the standard and the reference app write,** so any app with your seed finds what this one made.
- **When something can't be done, it says why, in one line.**

## Limits

- The app connects only to our connector, mcp.forest.foundation, for now.
- No push notifications: what's new shows when the app is open.
- While the seed is held, the page's own code reads it without asking; what keeps any other code out is the content security policy.
- Hiding the page doesn't drop the seed: a locked phone or a covered window keeps it, so someone who unlocks your device while it's held can act as you without the passkey asking.
- The host you write to can link your profiles and your passkeys: one device writes them all, one wallet funds them, one device reads all their inboxes. With the morning email on, it is also told they are one person's, and your email address. It promises to keep no network addresses in its logs; that's its promise, not the app's.
- To show your ratings, the app asks the index about each of your profiles from your device, so the index can link them, as the host already can; opening someone's profile asks it about theirs, so it sees whose profiles you look at.
- What's new goes by the time each writer puts on what they write: a review dated in the past shows no dot.
- With nothing unlocked, new messages show as a dot, never a count: until your seed opens them, the app can't tell how many conversations they make.
- Your checks lists an ID stamp's name as this device last read it in your Profiles folder, at an unlock; its page reads the rest there, with your seed.
- Privacy and Terms frame the site's own pages, which don't allow framing yet: until the site does, their frames show nothing.
- A web app's code comes from its server each time you open it, so you trust whoever serves it. It's open source, and you can run your own.
- Whoever holds any one of your passkeys holds the seed: each is as safe as the manager or key that keeps it. So does whoever reads your 24 words.
- Your first passkey can't be removed: it is your seed.
- Deleting a profile takes back nothing already read: whoever read its entries keeps what they read, and its inbox keeps what came until the host's keep days are over.
- Removing a passkey takes back nothing it already opened: whoever opened its seed entry has the seed, and so does anyone who opens a copy a host still keeps.
- Where a passkey lives shows only on the device that added it, and only when the passkey says which it is; a name you give it shows only on the device you named it on; elsewhere it shows as Passkey.
- The app can't ask the host whether the morning email is still on. Stopped with the email's Stop link, or its profiles taken by another email address, it still says Every morning until your next Save finds out.
- Email lists the profiles this device lists. One made on another device and not listed here yet isn't shown, and a Save here leaves it out of the count.
- Anyone can see that your Profiles folder holds names you gave, and about how many from the entry's size, though not whose or what they are.
- I'm new makes a new Forest: someone who taps it after closing the passkey window by mistake makes a second seed, and their next visit's Start offers to delete that passkey, as one never verified.
- One host, ours, for everything you make, and only profiles on it can be messaged from here.
- Anyone can see when one of your profiles keeps a copy of what it sent, though not what it says or whom it's to; someone watching the other person's inbox could match the times.
- A circle's centre moves by at most a tenth of its radius: a 1 km circle put on your door still says which streets, so put it on your neighbourhood, as the form's hint says.
- A place's name comes from places, which knows towns and city sections everywhere, and Mexico's colonias; elsewhere a neighbourhood may show as its town, and with places down, a place shows by its size alone.
- The map's tiles come from OpenFreeMap, which, like the network in front of it, sees the area you look at.
- Photos show as the host serves them; the app doesn't check them against their names yet.
- Not yet: payments between people, carrying your reputation from one profile to another, moving to another host.
- A proof takes a few seconds on a phone.
- A check whose answer is lost after its hour leaves its stamp without its list for good: the stamp comes back by its number, but its facts can't be shown.

## FAQ

**What if I lose my passkey?**
Start on any device with another of your passkeys: every one opens the same profiles. Or, when Start finds nothing, tap I already use Forest, then Type my 24 words, and the app makes a new passkey for them. Add one in You's Passkeys, in another password manager, on a second phone or on a security key, and remove a lost one there. Lose every passkey and your 24 words, and nobody can open your profiles again, not even the foundation.

**Is it free?**
The app and the face check are; the ID check is $5, one time, held on your card and charged only if it passes. The host charges for what you write, a tiny fraction of a cent per listing or message, and your verification comes with a gift that covers thousands of them. Buying more comes later.

**Why do I unlock to post, but not to look?**
Posting needs your seed, to sign as your profile, and your passkey gives it only after you unlock. The app then holds it a few minutes, so posting again soon asks nothing. Looking needs nothing: everything your profiles show is public, and your device remembers which ones are yours. Messages are the one thing locked to you, so opening them takes an unlock, unless the seed is held.

**Who sees my face?**
Didit, the provider our verifier uses, and for the ID check, your document too. The verifier reads the result and turns your selfie into numbers; those, and for the ID check every field Didit read on your document, come to you in the list beside your stamp, which the app keeps locked in your Profiles folder. The verifier keeps only a one-way number made from that list, and deletes the check at Didit an hour later; Didit keeps a template of your face so the same face can't be stamped twice. The app never sees any of it, and your profiles show only how you're verified. The details: [services/verifier](https://github.com/foundationforest/services/blob/main/verifier/README.md).
